\paragraph{What a verification claim should contain.}
A common way of supporting an FSI code is a figure or table showing agreement with a published benchmark value, often the summary values of~\citet{Turek2006}, at one or two resolutions.
The studies of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_suite} suggest that such agreement is weak evidence, and that a verification claim should state at least five things, each of which mattered at least once in this suite.
(i) The reference, its source and its category: the summary and tabulated Featflow values of FSI3 differ by $18\%$ in one QoI, and of the ten problems without an exact reference five have a reference that is both independent and backed by its own convergence evidence (category B), only two of them published values (Section~\ref{sec:published_reliability}).
(ii) At least three levels for each error source, with space and time refined separately: further levels changed the conclusions drawn from two or three in FSI1, FSI3 and the 3-D pressure-pulse tube, and scaled refinement hides which error dominates (Section~\ref{sec:cross_convergence}).
(iii) Several QoI types, with the claim limited by the slowest-converging one (Section~\ref{sec:qoi_dependence}).
(iv) The iterative error: the form of the coupling convergence test, the residual reduction at acceptance and a tolerance check at the finest resolution, since a test normalised by a large total quantity accepted unconverged steps in every benchmark audited (Sections~\ref{sec:convergence_method} and~\ref{sec:iterative_errors}).
(v) The code version, toolchain and platform, with inexpensive cross-platform, decomposition and symmetry checks, since two of the defects found here could not be seen on a single platform or on a single mesh level (Section~\ref{sec:defects}).
None of these items requires a new benchmark, and all can be added to existing studies at a cost that is small compared with the refinement study itself.

\paragraph{Exact references are diagnostic, not only decisive.}
The category-A problems are among the least expensive in the suite, yet they did most to locate errors.
In the Womersley tube the exact reference traced a falling temporal order to an $O(\Delta t)$ end-flux inconsistency, separated from the coupling by fluid-only runs with the exact wall motion (Section~\ref{sec:womersley}); in the manufactured solution, fluid-only and solid-only runs against the exact fields located the first-order errors in the field solvers rather than in the coupling (Section~\ref{sec:mms_fsi}); in the ring added-mass problem the wet/dry frequency ratio separates the added mass from the solid and temporal errors, and the remaining $-0.13\%$ was attributed to the BDF2 phase error (Section~\ref{sec:ring}).
Neither diagnosis could have been made from convergence data alone.
A linearised exact solution is, however, exact only in the limit of vanishing amplitude.
At finite amplitude the computed fields live on a deformed mesh, and where the reference is evaluated (at the undeformed or the current cell positions) matters at $O(\eta\,\partial u/\partial r)$ next to a moving wall.
In the companion solver study~\citep{CardiffBatistic2026QM}, at the amplitude of the Womersley tube case this choice alone changes the near-wall reference velocity by up to $8\times10^{-4}$ of the peak, depending on how the fluid mesh is moved, which is comparable with the finest-mesh errors reported here; at a tenth of the amplitude it falls below them.
Exact references for moving-boundary problems should therefore be used at amplitudes small enough that this ambiguity is below the errors to be measured, and the evaluation point should be stated.

\paragraph{Model-form differences in code-to-code comparison.}
An independent code tests the shared discretisation error that same-lineage comparisons cannot, but only if the two codes solve the same problem or the difference is quantified.
In the collapsible channel the oomph-lib beam and the continuum wall differ at $O(h/L)$, quantified as $0.05\%$, which is why that reference is category B (Section~\ref{sec:collapsible}); in the FEBio ring comparison weak compressibility, finite strain, finite amplitude and the mesh-motion regulariser were each quantified, at the level of $0.01\%$ of the frequency ratio (Section~\ref{sec:ring}).
In the Mok lid-driven cavity the published Kratos example solves a different problem, with openings that move with the mesh; with the openings fixed, the remaining difference from solids4foam is the outlet condition, quantified at $0.5$--$1.7\%$, which limits that comparison to about $1\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:mok}).
In the 3-D pressure-pulse tube, by contrast, the Hookean wall studied here and the St Venant--Kirchhoff walls of the published calculations differ at a peak hoop strain of $\approx3\%$, and the references use BDF1 without time-step studies; there, the difference in time integration alone reproduces most of a 20--25\% discrepancy between published solutions (Section~\ref{sec:3dtube}).
A parameter of the independent code can also belong to its discretisation rather than to its solution procedure.
In the SU2 solutions of Turek--Hron FSI1 and of the ring added-mass problem, the artificial-compressibility factor of the incompressible solver sets the preconditioned wave speed in the upwind dissipation of the pressure--velocity coupling, and so changes the converged discrete solution, not only the convergence path: between two values of the factor, the drag of the steady rigid-flag problem CFD1 on the coarsest FSI1 mesh changes by $5.4\%$ and the shedding lift amplitude of the rigid-flag problem CFD3 on the 2x mesh by $23\%$, and the amplitude loss of the ring changes about fourfold while its frequency ratio moves by $0.01\%$ (\texttt{paper-data/fsi1\_su2\_calculix/README.md}, \texttt{paper-data/ring\_added\_mass\_su2\_calculix/README.md}).
Where it was measured under refinement (the ring) the effect decreases, so it is a discretisation error rather than a model-form difference; such a parameter must be held fixed through a refinement study and reported with the reference, as a stabilisation parameter would be.
A code-to-code difference can be interpreted only where it exceeds the sum of both codes' numerical uncertainties and the quantified model-form contribution.

\paragraph{Verification and validation.}
The Hessenthaler flap shows why the two are kept apart.
Refinement moves the computed tip from below the measurement to above it (from $-0.16$ to $+0.14\,\mathrm{mm}$ from L1 to L2 with the acceleration interface condition, and from $-0.10$ to $+0.31\,\mathrm{mm}$ from L1 to L3 with the zero gradient), so the close agreement of any one mesh is partly a cancellation of discretisation error (Section~\ref{sec:hessenthaler}).
A validation statement made on such a mesh would credit the model with an accuracy that belongs to the discretisation error.
Validation requires a verified solution with a stated numerical uncertainty and an experimental uncertainty; here the second is not available, and the first is not either, because the fluid-mesh sequence of the tip is not shown to be in the asymptotic range.

\paragraph{Limits of the suite.}
The suite covers incompressible, laminar, Newtonian flows coupled to elastic solids through a sharp interface, conforming or non-conforming.
It does not cover compressible or non-Newtonian fluids, turbulence, contact, free surfaces, or immersed and fictitious-domain interface treatments, and its hyperelastic solids are limited to St Venant--Kirchhoff and neo-Hookean laws.
All calculations come from one code family (partitioned finite volume, solids4foam). Independent numerical solutions with their own convergence evidence exist for the collapsible channel (oomph-lib), Turek--Hron FSI1 (Featflow and SU2--CalculiX), Turek--Hron FSI3~\citep{Failer2020}, the ring added-mass problem besides its exact solution (FEBio and SU2--CalculiX), the Mok lid-driven cavity (Kratos) and the flexible-bottom cavity (SU2--CalculiX and FEniCSx; COMSOL converges at first order to a value $2\%$ from the others).
Independent Kratos solutions of the 3-D pressure-pulse tube (two levels) and the beam in cross-flow (three levels) are not shown to be converged, and the remaining cases without an exact reference rest on self-convergence and on published values of incompletely quantified accuracy.
The three-dimensional problems are the least completely verified, for the cost reasons of Section~\ref{sec:cost}.
These limits mark where the suite can support a claim; they are not a judgement on the problems outside it.

\paragraph{Recommendations to benchmark authors.}
Most of the difficulties met in Section~\ref{sec:published_reliability} lay in the reporting rather than in the computations.
A benchmark is most useful for verification when its authors tabulate the QoIs on every level computed, not only the finest; report separate mesh and time-step studies, stating the time integrator and step; define the problem unambiguously, including the monitoring points, the inflow and any ramp; state how periodic statistics are taken (window, extrema or Fourier coefficient) and whether the solution is periodic over that window; and provide the reference values and, where possible, the case set-up or driver in machine-readable form.
The infrastructure of Section~\ref{sec:infrastructure} is one way of doing this.
