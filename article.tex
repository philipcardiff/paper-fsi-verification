\abstract{
Many benchmark problems exist for fluid--solid interaction (FSI), but few come with a benchmark solution of established numerical uncertainty, and agreement with a published value is often taken as evidence of correctness.
This paper assembles an open, executable verification suite for continuum FSI in which every reference is classified by quality (analytical, independently converged numerical, published but incompletely quantified, or weak) and the spatial, temporal and iterative errors are assessed separately.
The suite comprises two analytical problems, an added-mass ring and a thick-walled Womersley tube; a new closed-form manufactured solution for transient arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian FSI with a finite-strain solid and exact traction continuity at constant viscosity; and widely used problems (Turek--Hron, a three-dimensional pressure-pulse tube, a collapsible channel, a lid-driven cavity), several with independent solutions from other codes.
Against the exact and independent references, a nominally second-order partitioned finite volume code converges at close to second order for most quantities, but not all: the manufactured solution reveals first-order pressure and solid-displacement errors that the linear exact problems cannot, and the Turek--Hron FSI3 displacements are not yet in the asymptotic range. \hl{[FSI3: ring+box, to be replaced]}
Published references disagree for identifiable reasons: much of a 20--25\% discrepancy in the pressure-pulse tube is reproduced by first-order time integration, the printed inflow of a three-dimensional beam benchmark is inconsistent with its reference values, and the frequently quoted FSI3 values differ from the detailed reference tables by up to 18\%.
A common coupling convergence test can accept unconverged steps.
The cases, drivers and machine-readable references are released.
}

\section{Conclusions} \label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Draft of 2026-11-02; numbers repeated from Sections benchmark_suite and
% cross_benchmark.
An open, executable verification suite for continuum FSI has been assembled, with each reference classified by its quality and the spatial, temporal and iterative errors studied separately for a partitioned finite volume code.
The main conclusions are:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Where the reference is exact or independent, the code converges as designed.} In the ring added-mass problem and the Womersley tube most quantities converge at observed orders close to two; the frequency error of the ring at a practical resolution ($-0.13\%$) is the BDF2 phase error, and the finest amplitudes and profile of the Womersley tube lie within $0.12\%$ of the exact solution ($0.2\%$ for the attenuation; wall-displacement phase within $1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$); in the collapsible channel the error against oomph-lib extrapolated to zero mesh size falls at orders 1.6--2.5 to $0.33\%$ of the peak; the $0.5\%$ floor seen against oomph-lib at its tutorial resolution is that reference's own first-order spatial error.
	\item \textbf{An exact reference at finite deformation qualifies this.} A closed-form manufactured solution for transient ALE FSI with a finite-strain neo-Hookean solid, normal and tangential interface motion and exact traction continuity at constant fluid viscosity, which needs neither an interface source nor a manufactured material field and, to our knowledge, is the first such solution applied to a cell-centred finite volume code, exposed what the linear exact problems could not. The partitioned fluid pressure converges at first order (velocity at $1.2$--$1.4$); the fluid alone reproduces the coupled errors, so the cause is the segregated fluid, not the coupling: its outlet with a prescribed velocity gradient and pressure produces the velocity error at the outlet corner, and the time-derivative correction of its momentum-interpolated face flux makes the pressure error grow as the time step falls, while without it the interior pressure converges at second order and a first-order layer at the outlet remains, of open cause. The partitioned solid displacement is first order at early times, from a start-up error in time of the displacement-form solid, and falls to order $0.8$ on the finest pair, matching an $O(h)$ error in the deformed traction area of the total Lagrangian solids. IQN-ILS either stagnated or inverted the solid. A quasi-monolithic coupling, at close to second order with an orthogonal interface mesh, falls to about first order in the interface traction when the interface tilts, because its boundary pressure omits the non-orthogonal term; with corrections under review it converges at second order in space and time.
	\item \textbf{The quality of the reference, not the popularity of the problem, sets what can be verified.} Of the ten problems without an exact reference, four have a reference that is both independent and backed by its own convergence evidence (category B; five with the flexible-bottom cavity \hl{[pending merge of the SU2 data]}), and only two of these are published values; the two published sets of FSI3 values differ by $18\%$ in one QoI.
	\item \textbf{Two levels are not enough.} Third and fourth levels reversed conclusions drawn from two: with the baseline set-up the FSI3 displacements converge at an observed order of about one, and with a corrected set-up they still change by 1--2\% at $7.5\times10^5$ fluid cells, so a difference from the reference of that size cannot yet be attributed to either code. \hl{[FSI3: ring+box, to be replaced]}
	\item \textbf{Verification exposed defects that agreement with a reference did not.} An $O(\Delta t)$ end-flux inconsistency, a compiler-dependent aliasing error, a mesh-dependent interpolation degeneracy, a coupling test that accepts unconverged steps, a face-flux time-derivative correction whose error grows as the time step falls, and the start-up and traction-area errors of the total Lagrangian solid were all found by convergence, cross-platform, residual or component-isolation checks, none by comparison with a published value.
	\item \textbf{The iterative error must be measured, not assumed.} A coupling test that takes the smaller of two residual measures accepted unconverged steps in every benchmark audited, and in a quasi-steady case produced a drift larger than the spatial differences under study, which a steadiness check on the QoI did not detect; requiring every residual measure to meet the tolerance removes it.
\end{itemize}

Several directions remain:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Independent converged references where none exists}, above all for the 3-D pressure-pulse tube, whose independent Kratos solution has two levels and is not converged in the peak radial displacement, and the Hessenthaler flap, which has none; they need their own mesh and time studies, as the Kratos solution of the Mok lid-driven cavity has.
	\item \textbf{Further levels where the evidence stops at two or three}: Turek--Hron FSI2, and a fourth fluid level for the Hessenthaler flap.
	\item \textbf{Completion of the provisional cases}: the beam in cross-flow (a fifth mesh, level 4), the flexible-bottom cavity and the blob in treacle (an independent reference for the redefined case).
	\item \textbf{Wider coverage}: compressible and non-Newtonian fluids, contact and free surfaces; the companion paper~\citep{CardiffBatistic2026QM} applies the same suite to a quasi-monolithic coupling.
	\item \textbf{Higher-order discretisation}, for which the exact references of the suite can measure orders above two.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Supplementary material: the beam in cross-flow and its published inflow}
\label{app:beam}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\citet{Richter2012} (Section~6.2; Section~7.2 of the preprint) defines a steady three-dimensional benchmark: a duct $(0,1.5)\times(0,0.4)\times(-0.4,0.4)\,\mathrm{m}$ with a St Venant--Kirchhoff plate $(0.4,0.5)\times(0,0.2)\times(-0.2,0.2)\,\mathrm{m}$ clamped to its floor ($E_S = 1.4\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\nu_S = 0.4$), fluid density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and kinematic viscosity $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, and a bi-parabolic inlet profile. The quantities of interest are the streamwise displacement $u_x$ at point A $= (0.45, 0.15, -0.15)\,\mathrm{m}$ and the drag $F_x$ on the plate. The published reference values, $u_x(A) = 5.95\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_x = 1.33\,\mathrm{N}$ with a stated accuracy of $1\%$ ($5.924\times10^{-5}\pm10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.327\pm0.01\,\mathrm{N}$ in the preprint), are extrapolated from the finest three of five uniformly refined levels, whose displacements converge cleanly ($4.93$, $5.57$, $5.85$, $5.908$ and $5.920\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$); they are often attributed to \citet{Richter2015} \citep[e.g.][]{Aulisa2018}.

\paragraph{An error in the published inflow.}
The printed inlet profile has a peak of $0.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so its area mean is $0.133\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the published text states a mean of $0.15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ ($Re \approx 25$), and the preprint a velocity of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with $Re = 40$. The three statements cannot all hold. The preprint's $Re = 40$ with the plate height ($0.2\,\mathrm{m}$) and $\nu_F = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ corresponds to a velocity scale of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the computations below reproduce the reference values to their stated accuracy only with a $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak, while the printed profile gives $1.8$ times those values. We therefore take the $0.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak of the printed profile to be an error in the published definition, and the case of \citet{Richter2012} to be the same profile with a peak of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The author has since confirmed that the reference computations used the bi-parabolic profile of the published definition with a peak of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ (T.~Richter, private communication, November 2026), which is the corrected inflow solved here.

\paragraph{Earlier calculations.}
An earlier version of the case set-up placed the plate $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ further downstream, sampled point A on the upstream face instead of mid-thickness, used a different peak inflow and a transient start-up, and so solved a different problem. A same-lineage calculation~\citep{Tukovic2018} on one polyhedral mesh with a $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak gives $u_x(A) = 5.93\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_x = 1.31\,\mathrm{N}$, but its transverse displacement at A, about twelve times ours, is consistent with the shifted plate.

\paragraph{Sequences.}
Both inflows were computed on the Richter geometry, on the half domain $z \le 0$ with a symmetry plane, by partitioned transient-to-steady continuation (IQN-ILS with the strict coupling test at $10^{-5}$, three PIMPLE outer correctors, a Newton solid) on four uniformly refined block-structured levels (\texttt{blockMesh}; fluid $8\,112$, $64\,896$, $519\,168$ and $4\,153\,344$ cells; solid $512$ to $262\,144$ cells, 8 to 64 cells through the thickness) at a fixed $\Delta t = 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$; $F_x$ is integrated over the wetted plate surface of the computed half (Table~\ref{tab:beam_seq}).
The non-spatial errors are small against the level-2-to-3 changes: coupling tolerances from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-8}$ change $u_x(A)$ and $F_x$ by at most $2\times10^{-6}$ (relative), starting from rest or from the converged rigid-plate flow by at most $1.6\times10^{-6}$, and halving $\Delta t$, which acts on the steady state through the Rhie--Chow interpolation, by $8\times10^{-5}$ (relative) in $u_x(A)$ on level 3 (printed inflow), about $1/240$ of the level-2-to-3 change ($2.0\%$).

With the corrected inflow, $u_x(A)$ and $F_x$ converge monotonically with observed orders rising from 1.3--1.4 (levels 0--2) to 1.6 (levels 1--3), as they do with the printed inflow; the asymptotic range is suggested but not demonstrated, so no error bound is given.
The comparison was made after the sequence was characterised: level 3 lies within $0.1\%$ ($u_x$) and $0.2\%$ ($F_x$) of the reference values, and conditional Richardson extrapolations from levels 1--3 lie $0.5$--$0.8\%$ above them, within their stated $1\%$; $F_y$ is within $0.3\%$ of the same-lineage value of \citet{Tukovic2018}.

\paragraph{An independent solution.}
A co-author computed both inflows with Kratos Multiphysics~10.3~\citep{Dadvand2010}, which shares no code with solids4foam (P1/P1 stabilised fluid on tetrahedra obtained by splitting the hexahedra of levels 0, 1 and 2, the finest with $3.1\times10^{6}$ tetrahedra, a total Lagrangian St Venant--Kirchhoff plate of trilinear hexahedra, Dirichlet--Neumann coupling with MVQN; \texttt{paper-data/kratos\_beam\_richter/}).
With the corrected inflow its $u_x(A)$ falls by $1.1\%$ from level 0 to level 1 and by $0.10\%$ from level 1 to level 2, to $5.970\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$, which is $0.44\%$ above our level 3 and $0.34\%$ above the reference value; its conditional Richardson value, $5.970\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ (apparent order $3.5$), is $0.47\%$ below ours ($5.998\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$).
Ours rises by $19\%$ from level 0 to level 1, because the force on the plate falls with refinement in Kratos and rises in ours, so the two codes approach the displacement from opposite sides.
With the printed inflow the level-2 displacement is $1.759$ times larger (ours: $1.758$), so an independent code also reaches the reference values only with the $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak.
Kratos gives two estimates of $F_x$, from the interface reactions and from the traction integral; their difference falls from $4.5\%$ on level 1 to $2.6\%$ on level 2, and they still bracket both our level 3 and the reference value.
The reaction estimate falls monotonically (apparent order $1.8$) to a conditional Richardson value of $1.3324\,\mathrm{N}$, which agrees with ours ($1.3325\,\mathrm{N}$) to $0.01\%$, and to $0.01\%$ also at the printed inflow ($2.3931$ against $2.3928\,\mathrm{N}$).
The independent force thus confirms our extrapolated force closely, while the extrapolated displacements of the two codes differ by $0.5\%$; with the apparent orders of $3.5$ and $1.8$ and a non-monotone traction estimate, the Kratos sequence is not shown to be asymptotic, and the reference category is unchanged \hl{[author decision: category with three Kratos levels]}.
The case is kept provisional until a level with consistent observed orders confirms the asymptotic range; a fifth mesh (level 4, about $3.3\times10^{7}$ fluid cells) is estimated at $340$--$930$ node-hours.
Levels 0 and 1 cost $158$ node-hours on MeluXina (128 cores per node) for both inflows, of which $53$ for the corrected inflow; level 2 ran on a shared workstation (wall times in \texttt{paper-data/kratos\_beam\_richter/README.md}).
