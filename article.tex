\abstract{
Many benchmark problems exist for fluid--solid interaction (FSI), but few come with a benchmark solution of established numerical uncertainty, and agreement with a published value is often taken as evidence of correctness.
This paper assembles an open, executable verification suite for continuum FSI in which every reference is classified by quality (exact, independently converged, partly quantified or unquantified) and the spatial, temporal and iterative errors are assessed separately.
The suite comprises two analytical problems, an added-mass ring and a thick-walled Womersley tube; a new closed-form manufactured solution for transient arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian FSI with a finite-strain solid and exact traction continuity at constant viscosity; and widely used problems (Turek--Hron, a three-dimensional pressure-pulse tube, a collapsible channel, a lid-driven cavity), several with independent solutions from other codes.
Against the exact and independent references, a nominally second-order partitioned finite volume code converges at close to second order for most quantities, but not all: the manufactured solution exposed first-order errors in the outlet flux of the fluid and in the start-up and traction area of the solid that the linear exact problems cannot, and with them removed the code converges at close to second order against it, except in the pressure of a Robin--Neumann coupling; the Turek--Hron FSI3 displacements are not yet in the asymptotic range.
Published references disagree for identifiable reasons: much of a 20--25\% discrepancy in the pressure-pulse tube is reproduced by first-order time integration, the printed inflow of a three-dimensional beam benchmark is inconsistent with its reference values, and the frequently quoted FSI3 values differ from the detailed reference tables by up to 18\%.
A common coupling convergence test can accept unconverged steps.
The cases, drivers and machine-readable references are released.
}

\section{Conclusions} \label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Draft of 2026-11-02; numbers repeated from Sections benchmark_suite and
% cross_benchmark.
An open, executable verification suite for continuum FSI has been assembled, with each reference classified by its quality and the spatial, temporal and iterative errors studied separately for a partitioned finite volume code.
The main conclusions are:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Where the reference is exact or independent, the code converges as designed.} In the ring added-mass problem and the Womersley tube most quantities converge at observed orders close to two; the frequency error of the ring at a practical resolution ($-0.13\%$) is the BDF2 phase error, and the finest amplitudes and profile of the Womersley tube lie within $0.12\%$ of the exact solution ($0.2\%$ for the attenuation; wall-displacement phase within $1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$); in the collapsible channel the difference from oomph-lib extrapolated to zero mesh size falls at orders 1.6--2.5 to $0.33\%$ of the peak; the $0.5\%$ floor seen against oomph-lib at its tutorial resolution is that reference's own first-order spatial error.
	\item \textbf{An exact reference at finite deformation qualifies this.} A closed-form manufactured solution for transient ALE FSI with a finite-strain neo-Hookean solid, normal and tangential interface motion and exact traction continuity at constant fluid viscosity, which needs neither an interface source nor a manufactured material field and, to our knowledge, is the first such solution applied to a cell-centred finite volume code, exposed what the linear exact problems could not. The partitioned fluid pressure first converged at first order and the solid displacement lost its order; fluid-only and solid-only runs traced this to the field solvers and their set-up, not to the coupling: an outlet face flux built from the cell rather than the boundary velocity, which gives an $O(h^2/\Delta t)$ pressure error, the lagged convection of a single outer corrector in fluid-alone runs, a first-order start-up of the displacement-form solid from rest under a non-zero acceleration, and an $O(h)$ error in the deformed traction area. With these removed, both partitioned couplings converge at close to second order from $n = 16$ to $128$ (with Aitken relaxation, fluid velocity and pressure at $1.7$--$2.1$, solid and interface displacements and interface traction at $1.7$--$2.3$); the Robin--Neumann pressure stays at $1.6$--$1.8$, as its interface pressure condition omits the viscous and body-force normal terms \hl{[pending: viscous term in the Robin pressure condition]}. A quasi-monolithic coupling, close to second order with an orthogonal interface mesh, falls to about first order in the interface traction when the interface tilts, since its boundary pressure omits the non-orthogonal term; with three unreleased corrections it converges at second order in space and time.
	\item \textbf{The quality of the reference, not the popularity of the problem, sets what can be verified.} Of the ten problems without an exact reference, four have a reference that is both independent and backed by its own convergence evidence (category B; five with the flexible-bottom cavity \hl{[pending merge of the SU2 data]}), and only two of these are published values; the two published sets of FSI3 values differ by $18\%$ in one QoI.
	\item \textbf{Two levels are not enough.} Further levels changed conclusions drawn from fewer: the FSI3 displacements agree with an independent reference to $0.3\%$ on the coarsest of three levels, but with refinement they move away from it, to $2\%$ in $u_x$ on $3.9\times10^5$ fluid cells, while their changes shrink at observed orders of three and above, so they are not resolved to the level of their difference from the reference; the change in the peak displacement of the 3-D pressure-pulse tube grows on its third level.
	\item \textbf{Verification exposed defects that agreement with a reference did not.} Defects in the boundary treatment, the field algebra, the interface transfer, the coupling test and the field solvers of the code under test were all found by convergence, cross-platform, residual or component-isolation checks, none by comparison with a published value (Section~\ref{sec:defects}; Appendix~\ref{app:lessons}).
	\item \textbf{The iterative error must be measured, not assumed.} A coupling test that takes the smaller of two residual measures accepted unconverged steps in every benchmark audited, and in a quasi-steady case produced a drift larger than the spatial differences under study, which a steadiness check on the QoI did not detect; requiring every residual measure to meet the tolerance removes it.
\end{itemize}

Several directions remain:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Independent converged references where none exists}, above all for the 3-D pressure-pulse tube, whose independent Kratos solution has two levels and is not converged in the peak radial displacement, and the Hessenthaler flap, which has none; they need their own mesh and time studies, as the Kratos solution of the Mok lid-driven cavity has.
	\item \textbf{Further levels where the evidence stops at two or three}: Turek--Hron FSI2, and a fourth fluid level for the Hessenthaler flap.
	\item \textbf{Completion of the provisional cases}: the beam in cross-flow (a fifth mesh, L5), the flexible-bottom cavity and the blob in treacle (an independent reference for the redefined case).
	\item \textbf{Wider coverage}: compressible and non-Newtonian fluids, contact and free surfaces; the companion paper~\citep{CardiffBatistic2026QM} applies the same suite to a quasi-monolithic coupling.
	\item \textbf{Higher-order discretisation}, for which the exact references of the suite can measure orders above two.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Supplementary material: the beam in cross-flow and its published inflow}
\label{app:beam}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\citet{Richter2012} (Section~6.2; Section~7.2 of the preprint) defines a steady three-dimensional benchmark: a duct $(0,1.5)\times(0,0.4)\times(-0.4,0.4)\,\mathrm{m}$ with a St Venant--Kirchhoff plate $(0.4,0.5)\times(0,0.2)\times(-0.2,0.2)\,\mathrm{m}$ clamped to its floor ($E_S = 1.4\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\nu_S = 0.4$), fluid density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and kinematic viscosity $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, and a bi-parabolic inlet profile. The quantities of interest are the streamwise displacement $u_x$ at point A $= (0.45, 0.15, -0.15)\,\mathrm{m}$ and the drag $F_x$ on the plate. The published reference values, $u_x(A) = 5.95\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_x = 1.33\,\mathrm{N}$ with a stated accuracy of $1\%$ ($5.924\times10^{-5}\pm10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.327\pm0.01\,\mathrm{N}$ in the preprint), are extrapolated from the finest three of five uniformly refined levels, whose displacements converge cleanly ($4.93$, $5.57$, $5.85$, $5.908$ and $5.920\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$); they are often attributed to \citet{Richter2015} \citep[e.g.][]{Aulisa2018}.

\paragraph{An error in the published inflow.}
The printed inlet profile has a peak of $0.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so its area mean is $0.133\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the published text states a mean of $0.15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ ($Re \approx 25$), and the preprint a velocity of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with $Re = 40$. The three statements cannot all hold. The preprint's $Re = 40$ with the plate height ($0.2\,\mathrm{m}$) and $\nu_F = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ corresponds to a velocity scale of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the computations below reproduce the reference values to their stated accuracy only with a $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak, while the printed profile gives $1.8$ times those values. We therefore take the $0.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak of the printed profile to be an error in the published definition, and the case of \citet{Richter2012} to be the same profile with a peak of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The author has confirmed that the reference computations used the bi-parabolic profile of the published definition with a peak of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ (T.~Richter, private communication, November 2026), which is the corrected inflow solved here.

\paragraph{A same-lineage calculation.}
A calculation with an earlier version of the code under test~\citep{Tukovic2018}, on one polyhedral mesh with a $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak, gives $u_x(A) = 5.93\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_x = 1.31\,\mathrm{N}$, but its transverse displacement at A is about twelve times ours, consistent with a plate placed $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ further downstream, so it is not a solution of the same problem.

\paragraph{Sequences.}
Both inflows were computed on the Richter geometry, on the half domain $z \le 0$ with a symmetry plane, by partitioned transient-to-steady continuation (IQN-ILS with the strict coupling test at $10^{-5}$, three PIMPLE outer correctors, a Newton solid) on four uniformly refined block-structured levels, L1--L4 (\texttt{blockMesh}; fluid $8\,112$, $64\,896$, $519\,168$ and $4\,153\,344$ cells; solid $512$ to $262\,144$ cells, 8 to 64 cells through the thickness) at a fixed $\Delta t = 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$; $F_x$ is integrated over the wetted plate surface of the computed half (Table~\ref{tab:beam_seq}).
The runs used a build without the interpolation-weight correction of Appendix~\ref{app:lessons} (\texttt{-O2}, so the aliasing defect does not apply), which cannot affect them: the conformal interface is transferred by direct face mapping, which does not use the barycentric weights, the smallest interface triangle ($1.2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ on L4) is 77 times the area below which the weights degenerated, and an L2 rerun with and without the correction gives bit-identical histories of $u_x(A)$ and $F_x$ over the first $1.9\,\mathrm{s}$.
The non-spatial errors are small against the L3-to-L4 changes: coupling tolerances from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-8}$ change $u_x(A)$ and $F_x$ by at most $2\times10^{-6}$ (relative), starting from rest or from the converged rigid-plate flow by at most $1.6\times10^{-6}$, and halving $\Delta t$, which acts on the steady state through the Rhie--Chow interpolation, by $8\times10^{-5}$ (relative) in $u_x(A)$ on L4 (printed inflow), about $1/240$ of the L3-to-L4 change ($2.0\%$).

With the corrected inflow, $u_x(A)$ and $F_x$ converge monotonically with observed orders rising from 1.3--1.4 (L1--L3) to 1.6 (L2--L4), as they do with the printed inflow; the asymptotic range is suggested but not demonstrated, so no error bound is given.
The comparison was made after the sequence was characterised: L4 lies within $0.1\%$ ($u_x$) and $0.2\%$ ($F_x$) of the reference values, and conditional Richardson extrapolations from L2--L4 lie $0.5$--$0.8\%$ above them, within their stated $1\%$; $F_y$ is within $0.3\%$ of the same-lineage value of \citet{Tukovic2018}.

\paragraph{An independent solution.}
A co-author computed both inflows with Kratos Multiphysics~10.3~\citep{Dadvand2010}, which shares no code with solids4foam (P1/P1 stabilised fluid on tetrahedra obtained by splitting the hexahedra of L1, L2 and L3, the finest with $3.1\times10^{6}$ tetrahedra, a total Lagrangian St Venant--Kirchhoff plate of trilinear hexahedra, Dirichlet--Neumann coupling with MVQN; \texttt{paper-data/kratos\_beam\_richter/}).
With the corrected inflow its $u_x(A)$ falls by $1.1\%$ from L1 to L2 and by $0.10\%$ from L2 to L3, to $5.970\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$, which is $0.44\%$ above our L4 and $0.34\%$ above the reference value; its conditional Richardson value, $5.970\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ (apparent order $3.5$), is $0.47\%$ below ours ($5.998\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$).
Ours rises by $19\%$ from L1 to L2, because the force on the plate falls with refinement in Kratos and rises in ours, so the two codes approach the displacement from opposite sides.
With the printed inflow the L3 displacement is $1.759$ times larger (ours: $1.758$), so an independent code also reaches the reference values only with the $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ peak.
Kratos gives two estimates of $F_x$, from the interface reactions and from the traction integral; their difference falls from $4.5\%$ on L2 to $2.6\%$ on L3, and they still bracket both our L4 and the reference value.
The reaction estimate falls monotonically (apparent order $1.8$) to a conditional Richardson value of $1.3324\,\mathrm{N}$, which agrees with ours ($1.3325\,\mathrm{N}$) to $0.01\%$, and to $0.01\%$ also at the printed inflow ($2.3931$ against $2.3928\,\mathrm{N}$).
The independent force thus confirms our extrapolated force closely, while the extrapolated displacements of the two codes differ by $0.5\%$; with the apparent orders of $3.5$ and $1.8$ and a non-monotone traction estimate, the Kratos sequence is not shown to be asymptotic, and the reference category is unchanged \hl{[author decision: category with three Kratos levels]}.
The case is kept provisional until a level with consistent observed orders confirms the asymptotic range; a fifth mesh (L5, about $3.3\times10^{7}$ fluid cells) is estimated at $340$--$930$ node-hours.
The Kratos runs on L1 and L2 cost $158$ node-hours on MeluXina (128 cores per node) for both inflows, of which $53$ for the corrected inflow; L3 ran on a shared workstation (wall times in \texttt{paper-data/kratos\_beam\_richter/README.md}).

\section{Independent computations of the ring added-mass problem}
\label{app:ring_independent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
This appendix gives the detail of the two independent calculations summarised in Section~\ref{sec:ring}.

\paragraph{FEBio: model.}
FEBio~v4.13~\citep{Maas2012,Ateshian2018,Shim2019} solves the ring problem with its fluid--FSI module, which uses the nodal fluid velocity relative to the mesh and the dilatation as unknowns, with equal-order hexahedral interpolation, a monolithic Newton solver and generalised-$\alpha$ time integration with no numerical damping.
The aim is to solve the same physical problem as the finite volume calculations, not an analytical limit of it: the same geometry, densities and $\nu_F=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, no-slip on the rigid wall and on the moving ring (imposed as zero relative fluid velocity on both), the same initial ring velocity, and the fluid initially at rest.
The discretisations differ: linear hexahedra for ring and fluid, on meshes generated by a script, mesh motion by a massless regularising solid with $E=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and a different time integrator.
Three model-form differences remain, which were quantified rather than removed: FEBio's fluid is weakly compressible, with bulk modulus $K=10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$; its solid is a finite-strain isotropic elastic material with the same $E_S$ and $\nu_S$, whereas the finite volume solid is small-strain; and the response amplitude is finite.
In the moderate level, reducing $K$ by an order of magnitude, to $10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, changed the wet/dry ratio by $-0.0011\%$ of the reference ratio, and increasing it by an order of magnitude, to $10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, by $+0.0063\%$.
Increasing the regulariser stiffness tenfold changed the ratio by $+0.0015\%$, and halving the amplitude changed it by $-0.0068\%$.
None of these changes removes the remaining difference from the finite-viscosity ratio.
Over the tested range, compressibility therefore changes the ratio by less than $0.01\%$, but the incompressible limit is not reached: the change from $10^{8}$ to $10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is larger than that from $10^{7}$ to $10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, so the sequence in $K$ does not converge towards a limit that could be extrapolated.

\paragraph{FEBio: resolution of the viscous layers.}
The Stokes layer, $\delta=\sqrt{2\nu_F/\omega}=2.9\times10^{-4}$, $3.3\times10^{-4}$ and $5.1\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}$ for the weak, moderate and strong levels, must be resolved by FEBio, since it imposes no-slip at the nodes.
A uniform fluid mesh of 12 radial cells gives a wet/dry ratio error of $-6.2\%$ against the inviscid ratio, whereas a first fluid cell of $\delta/4$ at both walls, used for all results below, gives $+0.001\%$ against the finite-viscosity ratio in the moderate level (potential-flow start); the graded-mesh ladder is in \suppRef.

\paragraph{FEBio: frequency and damping.}
With the layers resolved and the fluid starting at rest, the wet/dry ratio agrees with the exact finite-viscosity ratio to better than $0.010\%$ at all three levels, and differs from the inviscid ratio by $0.0010\%$, $0.031\%$ and $0.083\%$ (weak, moderate, strong); FEBio therefore reproduces the small viscous shift of the reference (Table~\ref{tab:ring_febio}, Figure~\ref{fig:ring_febio_ratio}a).
The fitted amplitude decay rate is $0.974$--$0.998$ of the exact viscous rate for all six runs; the displacement histories and the reconstructed interface mechanics are in \suppFigBoth.
Halving the time step changed the ratio, against the matched, equally refined dry ring, by at most $0.0003\%$ of the reference ratio, and doubling the ring and tangential resolution by at most $0.0007\%$; the absolute wet frequency converges at a difference-based order of about two, with the hexahedral ring supplying the common error, so these agreements are not limited by temporal or tangential resolution.
The fluid-at-rest start launches an acoustic transient in the weakly compressible fluid, and the ring and the fluid share momentum in the first step, so the response amplitude is about $1/(1+m^*)$ of $V_0/\omega$ ($0.89$, $0.49$ and $0.088$ of $V_0/\omega$, against $0.91$, $0.50$ and $0.090$).
The problem is linear, so frequency and decay rate are unaffected, and a start from the potential-flow field in the strong level gives a ratio error against the finite-viscosity value of $+0.04\%$ and a decay of $1.02$ times the exact rate.
Free-slip FEBio runs, an analytical-limit study with a different wall condition, agree with the inviscid ratio to $\leq0.041\%$ on the finest mesh.

\paragraph{FEBio and solids4foam.}
The finite volume fluid mesh does not resolve the Stokes layer ($\delta/h=0.009$ to $0.036$), so the solids4foam ratios are compared with the inviscid reference.
With the standard solid in the moderate level their errors ($+0.069\%$, $+0.018\%$ and $+0.0051\%$ for IQN-ILS at mesh factors 1, 2 and 4) are discretisation errors that fall with refinement, on the opposite side of the inviscid ratio from the viscous shift ($-0.026\%$ for that level).
The fitted solids4foam amplitude decay at 100 steps per period ($2.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, moderate, factor 2) is $4.2$ times the viscous rate and falls by factors of $3.3$ and $1.5$ with each halving of the time step from 25 to 100 steps per period, so it is dominated by temporal and coupling damping.
The largest difference between the FEBio and the finest-mesh solids4foam ratio errors is $0.09\%$ (strong level): the two discretisations agree at the level of $0.1\%$ in the ratio, and the viscous shift and damping are verified for FEBio only.

\paragraph{SU2--CalculiX.}
The inviscid problem was also solved with a fluid code, a solid code and a coupling library that are all independent of the code under test: the incompressible solver of SU2~7.5.1~\citep{Economon2016,Economon2020} in its inviscid form, with slip on the ring and on the rigid wall; CalculiX~\citep{Dhondt2004} for the ring (linear elastic, linear geometry, C3D8I hexahedra, Hilber--Hughes--Taylor integration with $\alpha = -0.05$); and preCICE with IQN-ILS coupling (\texttt{paper-data/ring\_added\_mass\_su2\_calculix/}).
The full ring is modelled, with fluid and ring meshes of $12F\times48F$ and $4F\times48F$ cells for refinement factors $F = 1$, $2$ and $4$, at 100 steps per period of the exact frequency, starting from the $n=2$ ovalling velocity with the matching potential flow.
On the finest mesh the wet/dry ratio errors are $+0.010\%$, $+0.042\%$ and $+0.052\%$ (weak, moderate, strong); they converge at observed orders of 1.91--1.92, and their Richardson values lie within $0.02\%$ of the exact ratio at all three levels, against $+0.001\%$, $+0.003\%$ and $+0.006\%$ for solids4foam with the high-order solid on mesh 4.
The dry CalculiX ring is not converged in space on the coarser meshes (frequency errors $+0.68\%$, $+0.09\%$ and $-0.06\%$ for $F = 1$, $2$, $4$), and the ratio removes most of this common error, as it does for the standard finite volume solid.
The time error is second order (observed orders 1.98 for the dry ring and 1.93 for the moderate level, on $F = 2$).
The amplitude loss on the finest mesh is at most $0.03\%$ per period, but it is not a clean measure of the coupled scheme: the solid integrator is slightly dissipative, and the loss depends on the artificial-compressibility parameter of the fluid, which changes it about fourfold on $F = 2$ between two values at which the fluid solve converges, while moving the ratio by only $0.01\%$.

The Supplementary Material consists of separate documents, each a cross-code check of one case by a second implementation; none is needed to follow the main text, and all were produced by the authors of this paper, so they are not independent references (Section~\ref{sec:ref_hierarchy}).
The first is a monolithic ALE finite element implementation (FEniCSx) of the collapsible channel on three spatial levels and four time steps, compared at equal time step with the oomph-lib and solids4foam solutions; it also replays the fluid load of one coupled run on the continuum wall and on a beam, which quantifies the model-form difference of Section~\ref{sec:collapsible}.
The second is a monolithic finite element solution (FEniCSx) of the St Venant--Kirchhoff, plane-strain flexible-bottom cavity with the exact interface traction on four refined meshes (Section~\ref{sec:cavity}).
The third and fourth are finite element implementations (FEniCSx) of the blob in treacle: one of the published small-strain definition, with an audit of its interface force transfer, and one of the finite-strain neo-Hookean case of Section~\ref{sec:blob}, with a cross-code check against solids4foam.
