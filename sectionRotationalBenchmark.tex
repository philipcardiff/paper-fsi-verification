\paragraph{What it tests.}
Both category-A cases of Section~\ref{sec:exact_refs} are linear, small-amplitude problems in which the interface transmits only a normal traction; neither has a rotational degree of freedom.
The radial--torsional ring adds (i)~a swirl velocity of the fluid, whose angular momentum is transported by diffusion and, with a radial through-flow, by convection on a moving domain, and a tangential interface traction (torque); (ii)~interface rotation and tangential mesh motion at finite amplitude; and (iii)~a nonlinear stretch--rotation coupling, since in a finite-strain compressible solid a finite twist forces a radial deformation of order $\theta^2$.
It does not add added-mass physics beyond ringAddedMass, and it is not tested for viscous wave propagation.
The problem is planar and rotationally invariant (in-plane rotation of an annulus), so that a two-dimensional case suffices.

\paragraph{Problem and reference.}
A neo-Hookean plane-strain solid annulus $A\le R\le B$ ($A=0.5$, $B=1\,\mathrm{m}$; $\rho_S=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $E_S=2\times10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\nu_S=0.3$) is surrounded by an incompressible Newtonian fluid $b(t)\le r\le c=2\,\mathrm{m}$ ($\rho_F=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $\mu_F=300\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$), $b(t)$ being the deformed interface radius (Fig.~\ref{fig:rot_geometry}).
The inner solid boundary is radially clamped and rotated, $\alpha_A=\theta_0S(t)\sin\Omega t$ ($\Omega=3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$); the outer fluid boundary is open with a prescribed pressure $p_c=P_0S(t)$, zero shear traction and the exact radial condition $\partial_rv_r=-v_r/c$; $S$ is a $C^\infty$ ramp over $1\,\mathrm{s}$.
Three members are defined by the loading: radial ($P_0=-3\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $\theta_0=0$), torsion ($\theta_0=0.1\,\mathrm{rad}$, $P_0=0$) and combined (both); results are given for $0\le t\le2\,\mathrm{s}$.

On the rotationally invariant ($n=0$) branch the fluid reduces exactly, at any amplitude, to $\mathbf{v}=(Q(t)/r)\,\mathbf{e}_r+w(r,t)\,\mathbf{e}_\theta$ and the solid to a one-dimensional problem in $R$, so the coupled problem is a system of one-dimensional partial differential equations.
It is solved by a spectral discretisation (Legendre modes for the solid, Chebyshev modes for the fluid) with Radau time integration; this is a semi-analytical solution of the reduced problem, not a closed form, and modes with $n\neq0$ are not part of it.
The evidence for it is independent of solids4foam.
(a)~The reduced equations and the hyperelastic stress were derived symbolically from the Cartesian map and agree with the implemented expressions exactly.
(b)~The linear limits reproduce the Bessel eigenvalues and the linear transfer function to $\le2\times10^{-10}$ and $4.5\times10^{-9}$, and the finite-amplitude static state agrees with a boundary-value solution to $10^{-14}$.
(c)~A second, independently written coupled solver (linear finite elements for the solid, finite differences on a mapped fluid grid, BDF time stepping; the reference response is not an input) converges at second order (error ratios $3.90$--$4.05$ per halving) for all quantities of the three members, with a Richardson-extrapolated difference to the reference of $\le1.8\times10^{-5}$ of the peak value.
(d)~Refining the reference itself changes the peak-normalised quantities by $\le3.2\times10^{-8}$ (stored reference against a solid resolution of 48 modes) and then by $\le2.5\times10^{-11}$ (48 against 64 modes, all three members), and tolerance, integrator and quadrature changes lie below this level.
(e)~In the nearly rigid limit $E_S=2\times10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ the reference agrees with a closed-form Stokes-layer solution (numerical Laplace inversion) to $5.9\times10^{-4}$ of the peak for wall torque and shear; its fluid angular momentum differs by $3.5\times10^{-3}$, which is not explained, and this check is not counted as support.
The nonlinear solid terms are therefore confirmed by derivation and by a second implementation, but that implementation is of low order and agrees to $10^{-5}$, not $10^{-11}$.
Because it has not been shown that the $10^{-11}$ plateau of~(d) is the round-off level, as an extended-precision calculation would show, the reference is classified as \emph{provisional category A} (Table~\ref{tab:ref_hierarchy}); the category describes the reference only and not the calculations below.
Four accuracies are thus kept apart: that of the semi-analytical reference ($\lesssim2.5\times10^{-11}$ against its own refinement, not shown to be round-off); that of the independently coded solver ($\le1.8\times10^{-5}$ against the reference, a statement about that solver); that of solids4foam (the errors of Table~\ref{tab:rot_floor} and below); and numerical acceptance and robustness (coupling acceptance, linear-solver criterion, failed or stopped runs), which are limitations of the calculations and say nothing about the reference.

\paragraph{Numerical set-up and comparisons.}
The calculations use the partitioned solver of Section~\ref{sec:numerical_methods} (IQN-ILS, BDF2, $\Delta t=2.5\,\mathrm{ms}$, two non-orthogonal correctors in the fluid) on three meshes of $64\times8\times16$ (M1), $128\times16\times32$ (M2) and $256\times32\times64$ (M3) cells (azimuthal $\times$ solid radial $\times$ fluid radial), refined by a factor two; M1 and M2 ran serially and M3 on four ranks.
Every formulation below is run on the same meshes and time step, so the orders are spatial at one $\Delta t$ and no temporal order is claimed.
\emph{Formulation~A} is the tutorial as stored: cell \texttt{leastSquares} for $\nabla p$ and $\nabla\mathbf{U}$ and the stock \texttt{newMovingWallVelocity}; every statement about solids4foam in this subsubsection refers to A unless another formulation is named.
\emph{Formulation~B} differs from A only in $\nabla p$, which is Gauss linear.
Research wall conditions used to probe the wall treatment (last paragraphs below) are not production code, and results are never transferred from one formulation to another.
Interface coupling was accepted on one relative residual measure alone for most steps (the legacy minimum test with tolerance $10^{-6}$): only $17$--$56$ of the 800 steps of any run satisfy both measures, and $93.0$--$97.5\%$ fail a both-measures test at $10^{-6}$ (A-test, Section~\ref{sec:convergence_method}).
The runs are consequently not claimed to be converged under a stricter coupling criterion. For formulation~A a partition and coupling-tolerance noise of about $2.4\times10^{-4}$ (torsion) and $1.3\times10^{-3}$ (combined) of the peak was estimated on M2 and limits the torque-type quantities ($T_B$, $\sigma_{r\theta}$, $T_A$) and their orders; none was estimated for formulation~B.

\paragraph{Torsion member.}
The errors of rotation, wall torque, shear traction, drive torque and angular momenta decrease monotonically against the reference, with observed orders $1.72$--$2.07$ (Table~\ref{tab:rot_floor}, Fig.~\ref{fig:rot_floor}; noise-propagated interval half-widths $\pm0.19$--$0.31$ for the torque-type quantities and $H_s$, sharp for $\alpha_B$ and $H_f$) and M3 errors of $0.08$--$0.2\%$ of the reference maximum.
These orders are consistent with second order, but are observed from three meshes at one time step and do not show asymptotic convergence.

\paragraph{Combined member: a reference-error floor.}
In the combined member the CFD solutions still converge nominally at second order against each other, resolved sharply for $\alpha_B$ and $H_f$ (self-difference orders $1.97$ and $1.94$), but the errors against the reference stop decreasing: the M2--M3 orders fall to $0.43$--$1.11$ and the M3 errors are $3.06$--$5.15\times10^{-3}$ of the peak, $1.7$--$4.1$ times the torsion values (Table~\ref{tab:rot_floor}, Fig.~\ref{fig:rot_floor}).
Self-convergence therefore does not imply convergence to the reference, in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:verification_methodology}, and only a reference of this accuracy exposes the difference.
Of the quantities of the combined member only these five show the floor; $H_s$, the wall angular velocity, the interface power and the solid kinetic energy lie above it and are not covered by this statement.
The floor was examined as follows.

\emph{The tested time steps and coupling tolerance do not explain it.}
On M2, with two-rank runs (a two-rank control reproduces the accepted serial run to three digits), halving and quartering $\Delta t$ leaves the $\alpha_B$ error at $5.141$, $5.142$ and $5.147\times10^{-3}$ and changes $T_B$ and $H_f$ by at most $5\%$; no temporal order is established for any quantity (the uncertainty of the order exceeds $1$).
Tightening the relative coupling tolerance to $10^{-7}$ and requiring both residual measures lowered the interface mismatch by three orders and changed the floor quantities by at most $3.6\%$ on the part of the run that completed; the run stopped at $t=1.825\,\mathrm{s}$ after 100 outer iterations and is kept as a failed run.
Within these configurations (M2, formulation~A, these time steps, this tolerance, $t\le1.825\,\mathrm{s}$ for the strict run) the completed tests do not account for the floor; they do not rule out a time-step or coupling contribution at M3, at other settings, or from a strict-coupling solution over the full interval, and they do not show that the accepted solution is accurate.

\emph{Changing the pressure-gradient operator reduces it.}
Changing only $\nabla p$ from cell \texttt{leastSquares} to Gauss linear (B against A) lowers the M3 errors of the five quantities by factors of about $1.7$ to $4$ (Table~\ref{tab:rot_floor}), with observed orders $1.8$--$2.4$ on the two mesh pairs of this sequence; three meshes do not show that the floor is absent asymptotically. The M3 errors of the interface power and of the normal traction $\sigma_{rr}$ fall from $4.8\times10^{-2}$ to $3.2\times10^{-3}$ and from $8.5\times10^{-3}$ to $8.9\times10^{-4}$.
In the torsion member the same change alters the rotational errors by at most $11\%$ (except $H_f$), so the torsion-only test does not reveal it.
In the coupled torsion runs a read-only budget diagnostic attributes the angular-momentum imbalance of the fluid to the torque of the cell-\texttt{leastSquares} pressure gradient (reduced by a factor of nine with Gauss linear); a synthetic polar-mesh example does not reproduce it, so the mechanism that generates the non-axisymmetric pressure content is not identified.
This is evidence of an operator sensitivity on one mesh sequence at one time step, not a claim that Gauss linear is better in general, that least-squares gradients are defective, or a recommendation to change the tutorial default; B is kept separate from the tutorial configuration A. The B runs fail the driver's linear-solver residual criterion at combined M3 (and at radial M2 and M3), for a finite-precision reason documented in the tutorial, and no noise estimate exists for them: the noise measured for the combined A runs ($1.3\times10^{-3}$) is of the size of the B torque-type errors, but it is not transferred to B.

\paragraph{Normal traction is not verified.}
The normal-traction quantities $u_B$, $\sigma_{rr}$, $p_b$ and the viscous normal stress $\sigma_{nv}$ do not converge at second order in A (observed orders $0.03$--$1.2$) and are excluded from order claims and from verification claims; they remain extracted and reported.
The evidence has different strengths.
Established by source inspection and a controlled research run is that the stock \texttt{newMovingWallVelocity} removes the wall-normal component of the velocity gradient, so that only $0.29$--$0.32$ of the reference viscous normal stress is transmitted in the combined member ($0.286$, $0.307$, $0.317$ on M1--M3), whereas a research wall condition that keeps the full gradient transmits it fully.
For the torsion member an inconsistency of the patch-normal vector used by the \texttt{leastSquares} gradient in OpenFOAM-v2512 on a twisted wall was shown on manufactured linear fields only.
The total normal traction satisfies $\sigma_{rr}=-p_b+\sigma_{nv}$ to $10^{-10}$ of its peak, and the errors of $p_b$ and $\sigma_{nv}$ are strongly correlated (correlation $+0.98$ in A, $+1.00$ in B) and cancel in $\sigma_{rr}$: on M2 of the combined member the $L_2$-in-time errors are $55$ and $39\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for $p_b$ and $\sigma_{nv}$ but $18\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for $\sigma_{rr}$ in A, and $37$, $39$ and $2.7\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in B.
A small total-traction error can therefore hide errors of both components.
A wall gradient constructed from continuity improves the reported $\sigma_{nv}$ by about a factor ten, but it makes the transmitted $\sigma_{rr}$ worse and an independent reconstruction of the stress from the solved fields does not improve, so a stress that is imposed by continuity is not independent evidence of the stress it imposes.
The wall conditions of this paragraph are research tools and not validated production changes.

\paragraph{Verification status.}
Table~\ref{tab:rot_scope} states which quantities the evidence supports.
The combined-minus-torsion terms ($16$--$22\%$ of the combined maximum; reproduced to $1.5$--$2.5\%$ of their size on M3, observed orders $0.2$--$1.4$) are a resolved signal but contain the response to the radial loading as well, so they are not the three-way interaction; the three-way term $u_C-u_R-u_T$ needs the radial member, which is incomplete (only M1 completed under formulation A; M2 stalled at $t=0.11\,\mathrm{s}$), and is not verified.

\paragraph{Lesson.}
The case shows two things that the linear cases cannot.
A reference accurate to far below the solver error separates self-convergence from convergence to the reference: the combined member converges nominally at second order and still carries an error floor, here strongly sensitive to a discretisation operator (the pressure gradient) to which the torsion-only test is nearly insensitive.
And a total traction can look accurate while its pressure and viscous parts are not, so that interface traction should be verified component by component whenever the reference allows it.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
	\item The reference is a reduced, rotationally invariant ($n=0$), semi-analytical model; its category is provisional and the stability of $n\neq0$ modes is not examined.
	\item Orders are observed from three meshes at one time step and one partition per mesh; no asymptotic or temporal order is established, and torque-type orders are noise-limited. The strict-coupling run failed at $t=1.825\,\mathrm{s}$.
	\item The floor attribution rests on one mesh sequence and three meshes; the Gauss-pressure runs have no noise estimate and do not pass the linear-solver criterion of the verification driver at the finest combined mesh, so it is not shown that the floor vanishes asymptotically.
	\item The normal-traction quantities, the three-way interaction and the radial member are not verified; the wall findings are research evidence and not production corrections.
	\item With no non-orthogonal correctors the M3 torsion run loses contractivity of the fluid iteration near $1.81\,\mathrm{s}$ (no root cause identified); the accepted runs use two correctors.
	\item The case and the reference kit are not yet archived with a persistent identifier; the runs take about 1.5 minutes (M1), 5.5--7 minutes (M2, serial) and 13--20 minutes (M3, four ranks).
\end{itemize}
Derived data of the supplementary results (acceptance counts, mixed terms, order intervals, floor by quantity) are in \texttt{paper-data/rotational\_*.csv}; the tutorial \texttt{radialTorsionalRing} (solids4foam PR \#637, draft) contains the case, the reference script, the verification driver and the supplementary figures.

\paragraph{Relation to existing analytical FSI solutions.}
Radial and rotational fluid--solid solutions have so far been given separately: \citet{WangPironneau2026} treat a radially moving linear-elastic disc and, in a separate problem, a rotating incompressible neo-Hookean annulus with a fixed interface; \citet{Serino2019} give a radial elastic piston and a rotating elastic disk with a linear solid at small amplitude; and the analytic solutions of \citet{Hessenthaler2020} concern axial shear without radial motion.
We know of no published solution of the simultaneous finite-amplitude radial and azimuthal motion with a moving interface (in the sources we accessed; this is an absence of evidence only).
Azimuthal shear of compressible nonlinearly elastic circular tubes has been analysed by \citet{Polignone1994}.
