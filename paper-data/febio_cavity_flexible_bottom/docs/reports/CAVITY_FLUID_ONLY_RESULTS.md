## Findings (what the data and FEBio source support)
1. With a nodal Dirichlet inlet (prescribed fluid velocity) the dilatation has alternating-sign spikes at the inlet nodes next to the wall corners (-1.3, +0.36, -0.10, ... decaying inward) that grow exponentially; even a straight channel fails. K, dt, Re (10 vs 100) and geometry do not remove it. Hypothesis (from `FEFluidVelocity.cpp`, not confirmed by an experiment): FEBio's inlet is the `fluid velocity` surface load, which also adds the inlet mass-flux term H*(v.n)*dA to the dilatation equation; a plain nodal Dirichlet inlet omits it.
2. That surface load cannot carry the parabolic profile through a `math` expression: the evaluated velocity was uniform = 0.02*ramp*(1.5 - 6*(0-1.5)^2) = -12*ramp, i.e. the expression saw Y = 0 (surface material points carry no position). It is the same class of defect as the v4.13 fluid-IC `math` problem seen in the ring work (per-node values are needed; here the load takes one vector per face and averages to nodes).
3. No flow-rate, pressure-gradient or interface-force check could be done because no run produced a physical solution. Straight-channel Poiseuille (dp/dx = -0.12 Pa/m, Q = 1) and the interface force (expected ~ -5.26 N/m) remain untested.
4. The inlet-profile workaround options I did not run (budget): (a) a face-constant velocity per inlet face, node-averaged (approximates the parabola with an O(h^2) ~ 1 % local error and conflicts with no-slip at the two wall-corner nodes); (b) a SurfaceData/`map` velocity (untested syntax in 4.13); (c) a locally patched binary (not allowed for results under the standing rule); (d) a prescribed-pressure inlet/outlet formulation, which changes the flow-driving mechanism and would need to be justified as a different but equivalent benchmark specification.

**Gate B0b: FAIL. The coupled pilot (B1) is not started.** Cavity simulations used: 7 of 8 (2 plate-only, 5 fluid-only). The remaining one cannot answer the question that blocks the pilot without choosing among options (a)-(d), which changes the setup.

## ADDENDUM (after the authorised final diagnostic): inlet audit and straight-channel Poiseuille run
Documents: `CAVITY_INLET_BOUNDARY_CONDITION_AUDIT.md` (read-only source audit), `CAVITY_POISEUILLE_DIAGNOSTIC.md` (the run). Run `runs/cavity/B0_poiseuille_normalvel`, Slurm 13977, pristine v4.13, 4 cores, 56 s.

Corrections to the findings above:
- **Finding 2 (Y = 0) is refined, not just "surface points carry no position".** In v4.13 `FEFluidVelocity::Update()` (nodal velocity) builds a default material point without `m_r0`, so a `math` profile is evaluated at the origin, whereas `FEFluidVelocity::LoadVector()` (the flux term) runs on Gauss points that do carry `m_r0`. The v3 inlet therefore had a uniform nodal velocity and a position-dependent flux term: internally inconsistent. This is a genuine v4.13 implementation limitation of that load for spatially varying `math` profiles, not a syntax error and not a generator defect. A face-wise `vec3` SurfaceData map is only face-constant (one vec3 per face), so option (a)/(b) would be an O(h^2) approximation with a node-averaged wall-corner value.
- **Option (a) (face-constant inlet) is withdrawn** in favour of the built-in `fluid normal velocity` load with `parabolic = 1` (+ rim pressure), which is the FEBio test-suite construction (`fl40`, `fs10`), uses one consistent nodal velocity/flux field, reproduces the planar Poiseuille profile exactly at the nodes of the slab, and leaves wall-corner nodes at zero with no conflicting constraint.

Straight-channel result (Q = 1, mu = 0.01, K = 1e6, 140 x 10 hex8): prescribed discrete inlet flux 1.0000000, outlet 1.0000017 (mass imbalance +1.7e-6), profile equals the discrete inlet parabola to 1.1e-6, **dp/dx = -0.1212124 Pa/m vs -0.1212122 from the actual flux (+1.6e-6)**; vs nominal -0.12 the +1.01 % is the known discrete-inlet approximation (nodal parabola of flux 1/(1-h^2)), accounted for before the run. Steady (gradient change 1e-12 over 10 s), 0 retries, no negative Jacobians, 0.062 core-hours. **Caveat:** a frozen odd-even pressure mode of 0.0124 Pa (0.73 % of the channel pressure drop, 1.02 x the per-cell drop) is present; it does not affect flux, profile or mean gradient, but must be quantified on the cavity interface before force comparison. The earlier nodal-inlet failures were not repeated at K = 1e6, so attributing them to the inlet formulation is supported by source analysis, not by a K-matched experiment.

**Gate update:** the *inlet treatment* is validated on a straight channel. The cavity fluid-only stage (cavity geometry, interface force vs ~ -5.26 N/m, Q conservation with the cavity) has **not** been run: all 8 budgeted cavity simulations are used (2 plate-only, 5 failed fluid-only, 1 passed inlet diagnostic). The coupled pilot remains not started.

Failed: **A3** (Fy_raw not steady: relative spread 8.2 % over the last 10 % of the run; the velocity probes ARE steady, 1.5e-6 to 2.9e-6, and Fx is steady to 1.2e-4) and **A4** (upstream dp/dx -0.1957 vs -0.12121, +61 %, polluted by the same drift).
**Diagnosis (from the logged fields only; no new run):**
- The pressure on the cavity floor drifts linearly in time (mean floor p 2.1, 8.0, 18.8, 32.0, 44.9 Pa at t = 5, 20, 100, 200, 300 s) and the raw floor force is -70.3 N/m with an odd-even amplitude of 149 Pa, against a physical O(1 Pa) pressure: **the raw pressure force is not usable**.
- The drift is confined to **43 of 1847 nodes, all in the two bottom corners of the cavity (x = 6, y = 0 and x = 2, y = 0)**, none elsewhere (0 drifting nodes with y >= 0.99; channel pressure is clean: centre-line p 1.77 Pa at the inlet falling to 0.08 Pa at x = 13.5). Rate of change over t = 200-300 s at the corner node (6,0): +641 Pa/100 s; its neighbours along the walls and diagonal follow the **geometric alternating series 641, -171.8, 46.0, -12.3, 3.3, -0.89 (ratio -0.268 = -(2 - sqrt 3))**, which is the signature of a single constant source at the corner node spread by the inverse consistent mass matrix. The left corner (2,0) behaves the same, with +28.6 Pa/100 s.
- Interpretation (supported by the signature, not proven by a controlled test): at a concave corner where every velocity DOF is fixed (no-slip floor and no-slip side wall), the dilatation row of the corner node contains only the free velocity of one diagonal neighbour, so the discrete steady-state mass balance at that node cannot be satisfied through momentum feedback and the dilatation grows without bound. A neutral nodal pressure mode forced by a constant source drifts linearly; the velocities remain steady because that mode barely couples to momentum.
- The same corner problem was not present in the straight-channel Poiseuille run (no concave closed corners there); there the frozen odd-even mode was 0.0124 Pa. The cavity interior-floor values are physically sensible (1.03-1.29 Pa for x in [2.4,5.3], mean 1.15 Pa; they do carry an odd-even component of about 0.15 Pa).
Consequence per the brief: **dependent FSI runs are stopped until the corner pressure is resolved.** An FSI model has the same corner nodes (the plate ends are clamped, so the corner nodes do not move), so the same drift would load the plate ends.
