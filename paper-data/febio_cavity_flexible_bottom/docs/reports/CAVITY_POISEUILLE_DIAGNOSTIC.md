# CAVITY FLUID-ONLY INLET DIAGNOSTIC: straight-channel Poiseuille (the 8th and last authorised cavity simulation)

Run `runs/cavity/B0_poiseuille_normalvel` (Slurm 13977, partition main, 4 cores, host a shared Slurm cluster node), **pristine FEBio v4.13 binary** (sha256 f5ae1ed6...72f02, the same binary as every other run), input `B0_poiseuille_normalvel.feb` (sha256 1a7d7b42...d4b), generated by `tools/make_cavity_fluid_feb.py --straight --inlet normalvel --k 1e6 --dt 0.5 --t-end 150 --ramp 10`. Analysis: `tools/poiseuille_check.py post` -> `runs/cavity/B0_poiseuille_normalvel/poiseuille_check.json`; figure `figures/cavity_poiseuille_diagnostic.png`. Inlet choice and its source-level justification: `CAVITY_INLET_BOUNDARY_CONDITION_AUDIT.md`. One simulation only; nothing else was launched.

## 1. Case (simplest valid case)
Stationary straight channel x in [0,14], y in [1,2], 140 x 10 x 1 hex8 cells (3102 nodes, 1400 elements, cells 0.1 m cubes, slab thickness 0.1 m in z, `wz` fixed on all nodes = plane flow). Fluid: rho = 1, mu = 0.01 Pa s (nu = 0.01, Re = U H/nu = 100 for U = 1), K = 1e6 Pa (Mach ~1.5e-3; dilatation ~1.7e-6, far above the ~1e-15 arithmetic floor), `rhoi = 0`, full Newton, dt = 0.5 s x 300 (150 s; slowest viscous mode decays as exp(-pi^2 nu t/H^2) = exp(-14.8) at the end), inlet ramp 0 -> 1 over 10 s. No-slip walls (`wx, wy` fixed on y = 1, 2), inlet: built-in `fluid normal velocity`, `velocity = -1` (mean speed 1, inflow), `parabolic = 1`, `prescribe_nodal_velocities = 1`, `prescribe_rim_pressure = 1`; outlet: zero dilatation (p = 0), velocity free. No fluid-solid interaction. Same inlet/wall/outlet construction as FEBio's official `fl40` (parabolic 2D rectangular inflow).

Independent analytical reference (geometry, viscosity, units re-derived): U = Q/H = 1 m/s, `dp/dx = -12 mu Q / H^3 = -0.12 Pa/m`, peak speed 1.5 m/s, pressure drop over 14 m = 1.68 Pa.

## 2. Pre-run accounting of the discretised inlet (done before the run, `tools/poiseuille_check.py pre`)
`FEParabolicMap` normalises the **linear interpolant** of the nodal field to mean 1. For the nodally exact parabola on h = 0.1 the interpolant's mean is 1 - h^2 of the continuum parabola's, so:
- prescribed **discrete inlet flux = 1.000000 exactly** (per unit depth, trilinear shape functions);
- nodal inlet speeds = continuum parabola of flux 1/(1-h^2) = 1.010101 (peak 1.515152, not 1.5);
- therefore the discrete solution must have `dp/dx = -0.12/(1-h^2) = -0.121212 Pa/m` (+1.01 % with respect to -0.12), not -0.12. Comparing against nominal Q = 1 would show a spurious +1.01 % "error"; it is not an error, it is the 1 % inlet approximation the brief asked me to account for.

## 4. Open findings (not hidden by the passes above)
1. **Persistent odd-even pressure mode (the one caveat).** The pressure has a sawtooth in x: even-indexed node columns sit on the line, odd ones lie lower by **0.01239 Pa** (even-minus-odd mean over x in [2,12], at y = 1.5; 0.01296 at y = 1.2, 0.01240 at y = 1.9), plus a smaller y-alternation of **0.00126 Pa** across a section (x = 7). That is 0.73 % of the inlet-outlet drop but **1.02 x the pressure drop of one cell**, so it is large against the local gradient (the gradient from adjacent single nodes is wrong; `centreline_p_monotone_decreasing = False`). It appears during the ramp (0.003 Pa at t = 5, 0.0128 at t = 10), then freezes: 0.012394258 Pa from t = 20 to 150, i.e. a neutral mode that is neither damped nor driven. It does not affect the flux, the velocity profile or the mean gradient (pair-averaged pressure is linear to 2.4e-7 Pa, gradient -0.1212123). Likely cause (**not tested**): a null mode of the equal-order hex8 velocity/dilatation discretisation, since the Galerkin x-gradient of an alternating nodal pattern vanishes on a uniform mesh. I did not isolate whether the inlet (rim pressure) or the ramp excites it: one run only.
2. **Outlet region.** Within the last ~0.7 m the pair-averaged pressure deviates from the line by up to 0.0126 Pa (the sawtooth anchored by the p = 0 condition at the outlet column); the pair-averaged line extrapolates to -0.0074 Pa at x = 14. Immaterial for the gradient (taken on x in [3,11]) but it means point values near the outlet are not clean.
3. **Late-time convergence tests are degenerate.** From about step 46 the Newton tables compare a vanishing increment with itself ("No force acting on the system"); 222 of the 300 steps (first at step 70) have no Newton iterations beyond the first table. The evidence of steady state is the solution itself (relative change of the gradient 1e-12 over 10 s, flux error 1e-6), not the iteration norms.
4. **Not a K-isolated result.** This run used K = 1e6; the earlier failed runs used 1e4 or 1e8 with the nodal and the `fluid velocity` + `math` inlets. A nodal-inlet run at K = 1e6 was not made, so "the old inlet failed because of the inlet" rests on the source audit plus the failures at two other K values, not on a controlled K-matched comparison.
5. **Straight channel only.** It tests inflow, no-slip walls, outflow and the Poiseuille balance. It does not test the cavity geometry (recirculation, the 40 x 10 interface block, the outlet block), convection (a fully developed flow has no convective term), the interface force, or the FSI coupling.

## 5. Decision: **PASS for the inlet treatment** (with the caveat in 4.1)
The built-in parabolic `fluid normal velocity` inlet with rim pressure delivers the prescribed discrete flux, a nodally exact planar Poiseuille profile, mass balance to 2e-6, and the correct viscous pressure gradient to 2e-6 against the *actual* flux (+1.01 % against nominal Q = 1, fully accounted for). The earlier fluid-only failure is therefore attributed to the inlet formulation (`CAVITY_INLET_BOUNDARY_CONDITION_AUDIT.md`), not to a general inability of FEBio v4.13 to solve this fluid problem. This is **not** a statement about the cavity FSI benchmark: the cavity geometry, the interface force and the coupling remain untested. The spurious pressure mode must be quantified on the actual wetted interface before any force comparison with the -5.257 N/m reference.
