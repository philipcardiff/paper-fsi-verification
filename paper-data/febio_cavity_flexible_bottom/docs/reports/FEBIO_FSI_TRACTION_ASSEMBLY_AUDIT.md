## 1. The two pathways, line by line
**Pathway 1: the load that moves the plate** (`FEFluidFSITraction`, FEFluidFSITraction.cpp)
1. `Activate` (l.82-118): per interface facet the adjacent fluid-FSI element `m_elem[j]` is the one of `el.m_elem[0/1]` whose material is `FEFluidFSI` (the plate material is St.Venant-Kirchhoff, so for our conformal interface it is the fluid element above the facet); `m_s[j] = FacePointing(...)` (+1/-1) flips `gr` so that `gt = gr x gs` points out of the fluid element, i.e. into the plate (FESurface.cpp:782).
2. `LoadVector` (l.157-196), per facet Gauss point (quad4, 2x2 rule, weights 1, FESurface.cpp:2417-2480):
   - geometry: nodal coordinates at `alphaf` (here alphaf = 1: current), `gr = d x/dr * m_s`, `gs = d x/ds`, `gt = gr x gs` (l.175-177). `|gt|` includes the Jacobian, so the weight x |gt| integrates the area.
   - **pressure:** `ef = sum_j H_j(xi_g) e_f(node j)` over the **4 facet nodes only** (`GetFluidDilatation`, l.121-133), `p = Fluid()->Pressure(ef) = -K ef` (l.185-186). Bilinear shape functions, the same 2x2 points: nothing is taken from the interior nodes.
   - **viscous stress:** `GetFluidStress` (l.136-154): `sv = (1/nint) sum_n Viscous->Stress(mp_n)` over the **8 Gauss points of the adjacent fluid element**, a single constant tensor per facet.
   - traction `f = gt*p - sv*gt` (l.189); nodal vector `H_a f` x weight is assembled on the **solid displacement DOFs** of the facet nodes (`m_dofU`, l.162). Tangent (l.199-295) includes `dp/de_f` and the viscous terms with the same sampling.
3. Hence, for a flat facet: `F1_y = -(sum over facets) A_f * [bilinear Gauss mean of nodal p on the 4 facet nodes]`: **it depends only on the interface-row nodal dilatation.**

**Pathway 2: plotted fluid surface force/pressure** (FEBioFluidPlot.cpp:164-226, 295-323)
- `FEPlotFluidSurfaceForce::Save`: per surface element the adjacent fluid element is found (`el.m_elem[0]`, else `[1]`), `s = mean over its 8 Gauss points of pt.m_sf` (l.202-212; `m_sf = viscous stress - p I`, FEFluidFSIDomain3D.cpp:875), `fn -= s * (SurfaceNormal(el,0,0) * FaceArea(el))` (l.217). Sign: the routine assumes the surface normal points out of the fluid; for our facets (normal out of the plate) the sign flips, the magnitude is the same. The element pressure is the **mean of the 8 nodal e_f of the adjacent element, i.e. the mean of the interface row and the first row above it** (and both z layers).
- The surface force is not requested in the cavity inputs (`<plotfile>` has displacement, fluid pressure, relative fluid velocity, fluid dilatation, reaction forces), so no FEBio-computed F2 exists; it is reconstructed from the stored element pressure.

## 2. Exact differences for our model (hex8, equal-order, 8-point Gauss, no interior stabilisation)
| | F1 (applied load) | F2 (plot definition) |
|---|---|---|
| pressure sampled at | facet nodal e_f (interface row), bilinear | adjacent element Gauss mean = mean of 8 nodal e_f (rows 0 and 1) |
| viscous | element Gauss mean of `sv` (not stored) | same element Gauss mean (as part of `m_sf`) |
| geometry | `gt = gr x gs` at 4 Gauss points, deformed | area vector at the facet (0,0) normal x face area |
| sees the row parity | **yes, fully** (wall row only) | **no first-order** (average of the two branches) |
**PROVEN (algebra):** F2 equals the pair-row mean F4 for planar facets: the facet integral of a constant element pressure `= A * (1/8) sum 8 nodes`, which is the facet integral of the bilinear field `(p_row0 + p_row1)/2`. Numerically F2 - F4 = 5e-8, -1.6e-8, 1.2e-9, 1.9e-9 N/m at M1, M2, M3, M3(717.5). **Consequence: the "pair-mean" diagnostic of `CAVITY_PARITY_MODE_CHECK.md` is not an independent estimator: it is FEBio's own element-average surface-force definition. That it reproduces -5.2494 and that solids4foam gives -5.2504 does not by itself show a source bug, and I do not claim one.**

### What each number does and does not say
- **STRONGLY SUPPORTED:** the load actually assembled for the plate (F1) contains the row-parity offset; F2/F3/F4 do not. Applied load minus the parity-free values: 0.72 (13 % of 5.44), 0.34 (6.5 %), 0.27 (5.1 %) N/m.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED:** which value is the true fluid force. F2/F3/F4 are parity-immune but they are one-sided averages of an unstabilised field; the agreement with solids4foam is: F2 against s4f same-dx QM = +6.1 % (M1), +0.9 % (M2), -0.02 % (M3). The M1 and M2 misses (6 % and 1 %, opposite sign to F1) show that "pair averaging reproduces the benchmark" is not a general identity; s4f itself changes by 2.6 % between dx 0.1 and its extrapolation. Observed F2 differences M1->M2 +0.1626, M2->M3 +0.0254 (ratio 6.4, order 2.7): F2 extrapolates to about -5.245 (HYPOTHESIS, three levels, M3 not steady to 1e-4); F1 extrapolates to about -5.00. They do not converge to each other as dx falls: F1-F2 = 0.720, 0.341, 0.266 (D = 0.365, 0.173, 0.134 Pa, decaying slower than h^2 from M2 to M3).
- **Viscous term of F1: NOT reconstructable from stored output.** It needs the Gauss-point velocity gradient of the adjacent element; the xplt keeps only element-average velocity. Bound from the audit (2-pt one-sided estimate on the same fields): |Fy,visc| = 0.0007 / 0.0012 / 0.0015 N/m (<= 0.03 % of F), same order for Fx (viscous force on the plate -0.0197, -0.0244, -0.0259). The sampling (element-Gauss mean) differs from that estimate; the bound is an order of magnitude only.
- Not reconstructable either: the plate reaction (xplt reaction forces are zero), the force during Newton iterations (only converged states are stored), per-Gauss-point pressure (only the element mean is stored; the element mean is all F2 needs).
### Uncertainty (numbers in the table)
state-to-state at M3 (t = 500 vs 717.5): F1 +0.0026, F2 -0.0025 N/m (0.05 %); quadrature of F1 (Gauss vs trapezoid): 1e-14; F2 vs F4: <= 5e-8 (identity); F3 vs F2: 0.0002-0.0010 (<= 0.02 %); parity split vs F1-F2: <= 0.0010; M3 is not steady to the 1e-4 criterion (u_y spread 1.05e-3, decaying 44 s plate mode) and all coupled runs ended abnormally, so every row is a conditional estimate. No weight or coefficient was adjusted to reach -5.25 anywhere.

## 4. A formulation-level observation on the discrete reaction (F5; not a force estimate)
Because the momentum row carries the pressure as `int N_i grad p dV` (no boundary integral), the Dirichlet-node reaction of the fluid equations does not contain the pressure traction: on the fixed-plate field C1b (M1) the sum of `-(A w + G p + conv)` over the floor nodes is -0.82 N/m (pressure-gradient part -0.8196, viscous Fx -0.0189), against -4.66 (F1) / -5.48 (F2). **PROVEN (algebra and the data above):** the pressure load on the interface must come from the surface routine; the monolithic coupling cannot supply a "natural" reaction check. This is a consequence of the chosen weak form, not a defect, and it means there is no internal consistency identity that would pick F1 or F2.
