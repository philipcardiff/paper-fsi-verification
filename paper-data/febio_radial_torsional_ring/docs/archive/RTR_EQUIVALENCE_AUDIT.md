## 3. Torque reconstructions defined in advance (per radian and per metre of depth: extensive value / (2 pi dz); sign: torque of the fluid ON the solid about +z)
All use logged nodal fields of the z = 0 layer (full double precision text log, not the float32 xplt): current node positions x, y, absolute fluid velocity (w for the fixed mesh, v_mesh + w for the coupled run) and dilatation e_f. The slab is z-uniform (checked with a top-layer probe line).
* **T1 (face-evaluated viscous shear torque from the element velocity gradients):** at the two in-plane Gauss points of every interface edge, evaluate the trilinear velocity gradient of the adjacent element ON THE INTERFACE FACE (not the element mean), form s = 2 mu D (+ the kappa term, zero) and integrate x cross (-s n_f) dl, n_f = normal out of the fluid. A wall-value traction (what a face-based method would apply).
* **T2 (F2: the traction as FEBio applies it):** the Gauss-mean of s over the 2x2 in-plane Gauss points of the attached element (z-uniform so equal to the 3-D mean), f_a = integral H_a (-s_mean gt) over each interface facet (gt the current facet area vector, outward from the fluid), torque = sum_a (x_a f_ay - y_a f_ax) over interface nodes. The nodal-pressure part p gt is reported separately (it carries no torque for an axisymmetric field; its value is a parity diagnostic). Identical algebra to `tools/hronturek_fsi_traction_recon.py` (the FEFluidFSITraction reconstruction used for FSI1), here with the exact quad4 facets.
* **T3 (angular-momentum balance, the weak-form reaction):** the torque the wall exerts on the fluid equals d H_f / dt minus the outer-boundary flux terms, H_f = sum rho int (x v_y - y v_x) dV with the exact hex8 quadrature (2x2x2 Gauss) and the polygon geometry; because the rotation field is in the FE space and has zero symmetric gradient, the viscous and pressure operators cancel (AE): T3 = -(dH_f/dt) + outer flux torque, dH_f/dt by central difference of the logged states. This is the consistent (energy-conjugate) torque and is expected to be O(h^2).
* Exact references: fixed geometry (RT-B): T_B = mu (b L_r - 2 L) at r = b from the independent spectral solver `tools/rtr_reference.py:fluid_exact` (N = 40 versus 56: 5e-9); coupled (RT-C): the proposal's reference.

## 4. Like-for-like verdict and differences
**A like-for-like comparison of the torsion member is ACHIEVABLE with stock FEBio** for every quantity that depends on the swirl: law (exact continuum identity), kinematics, geometry/mesh family (same polygon, same cell counts, hex8 versus finite volume), BCs (identical for the swirl including the open outer boundary and the radially clamped rigidly rotating inner circle), interface (conformal no-slip), ICs, QoI definitions (alpha_B, T_B, sigma_rt, H_f per radian per metre). **Differences (to be carried into every classification):** (1) weak compressibility K_f = 1e7 (declared, not swept); (2) hex8 equal-order velocity/dilatation fluid with element-mean interface viscous traction (first-order in h; the main expected discrepancy); (3) monolithic generalized-alpha rho_inf = 1, dt 0.01 (the proposal: BDF2, partitioned IQN-ILS, dt 0.0025); no time-step study will be run in this pilot; (4) three-field hex8 solid domain versus the s4f displacement discretisation (tested by RT-A); (5) the FEBio regularising skeleton (E_mesh = 1 Pa); (6) alpha_B is evaluated at the circle r = B (nodes) instead of the polygon face-centre radius B cos(pi/n_theta) (a 6e-4-level geometric correction at M1, noted, not applied); (7) no s4f data are reused: s4f numbers quoted come from the proposal's own CSV. **The README/input contradiction on the inner boundary is resolved in favour of radially clamped (section 1).**
