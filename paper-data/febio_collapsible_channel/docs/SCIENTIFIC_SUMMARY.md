# Scientific summary

**Status: DRAFT diagnostic evidence. The coupled FEBio-vs-oomph-lib comparison for the collapsibleChannel benchmark is NOT ESTABLISHED. Nothing here is a successful verification of FEBio or of the benchmark.** Stock FEBio v4.13 (binary sha256 `f5ae1ed6f347f875c392f93d847f2369f437345a9b785296d46b0bd6fbb72f02`, tag v4.13, clean), 6 Slurm jobs, 0.195 allocated CPU-h. Reference: oomph-lib solutions of the solids4foam collapsibleChannel verification suite (category B, precision about 0.3 % of the 0.216 m peak).

## Question
Does a monolithic hex8 `fluid-FSI` model of the collapsible-channel benchmark (pressure-driven channel, thin SVK wall with a 200 Pa follower external pressure, ramp 0.25 s) reproduce the oomph-lib wall-midpoint history (first trough -0.2160 m at 0.4685 s)? The pilot was designed in stages with pre-registered gates so that cheap tests could stop the expensive run.

## Findings
1. **Equivalence (audit).** Everything physical (geometry, SVK law, clamped ends, follower load, IC, pressure-driven ends, outputs) can be set up in stock v4.13. Irreducible differences: weak compressibility K = 1e7 ((omega L/c)^2 = 0.4 % [A]), the equal-order hex8 pressure operator, hex8 wall discretisation, monolithic generalised-alpha vs partitioned IQN-ILS/BDF2.
2. **Static wall (CC-S1; PROVEN data).** Stock hex8 at the tutorial wall mesh (80x8, aspect 20) is 11.6 % too stiff at the midpoint: DIAGNOSTIC DISCREPANCY. Convergence is second order in the in-plane spacing (ratios 3.9, 4.0) to the beam value; 160x4 misses its 3 % gate by 0.054 pp (-3.054 %); 320x8 is at -0.757 %: QUALIFIED AGREEMENT (one code, reference is a beam model). Amendment A1 therefore moved to the 320x8 wall and a 7,040-cell fluid mesh (tutorial: 3,200); my review of A1 is in `PLAN_AMENDMENTS_AND_ACCEPTANCE.md` (acceptable, with stated qualifications). The reference static value was re-derived with an independent beam code to about 0.05 %.
3. **Fixed-shape fluid (CC-F1).** Mass balance 6.0e-7, steady drift 1e-11, streamwise parity 6.8e-5 of the smooth variation: all pass their own gates (QUALIFIED AGREEMENT, one mesh, no external reference). The pre-registered force gate **failed**: nodal wall pressure (what FEBio applies) vs element-mean pressure differ by -6.99 % in F_y and -8.63 % in the first-mode load (stop value 8 %), through a wall-normal row-parity mode (wall row and row 2 form one family, row 1 the other; D = p0 - 2 p1 + p2 rms 1.75 Pa) that the streamwise parity gate cannot see.
4. **Dynamic diagnostic (D1, 20 coupled steps).** Hypothesis H_D (the spread is negligible in the dynamic collapse) **refuted**: nodal vs element-mean load spread +19 to +65 % in the loading phase (0.10-0.30 s), with row-1 pressure 77 Pa against 193 Pa on the other rows at x = 10, t = 0.25 s. A free-body identification from the wall balance reproduces the nodal load to 0.1-1 % for t = 0.225-0.35 s, while the element-mean value is 27-76 % lower (valid only for |u| <= 0.1425 m). The FEBio wall lags oomph-lib by up to 12.8 % of the peak (t = 0.425 s) and has not reached its trough at 0.5 s.
5. **Interpretation.** The lag has the sign and size given by the analytical load-balance law for a one-signed 25-60 % amplification of the wall load ([A], `LOAD_BALANCE_ANALYSIS.md`); this is consistent with, not proof of, the parity mechanism as the sole cause (single mesh and time step, rhoi 0.9, E_mesh 1, wall 0.76 % too stiff). Which of the two pressure reconstructions is physically correct is UNRESOLVED.
6. **Consequence.** By the pre-registered stop rule no full 140-step run was made. First trough value and time, trough-to-trough period (reference: 0.65 s), rebound and second trough: NOT ESTABLISHED. The same pressure-parity mechanism class as in PRs #27 (cavityFlexibleBottom) and #28 (FSI1) is indicated; its cause in this geometry is not shown to be identical.

## Direct output vs reconstruction vs estimate
Direct solver output (logged node data): displacements, nodal dilatation (pressure = -K e_f), nodal fluid velocities, Newton counts. Reconstructions from them: F1 (nodal wall pressure, the applied-load equivalent), F2 (element mean), G, free-body pressure, volume balance. Analytical estimates: the load-balance law, the beam re-derivation of the static reference, the Euler-type sensitivities. Hypotheses: that parity is the sole cause of the lag; that F2 is closer to the physical load.

## What would change the conclusion
A fluid operator without wall-normal pressure parity passing the same F1 and dynamic-spread gates (`RECOMMENDATION_AND_REOPENING.md`). No stock FEBio source change and no manuscript change is proposed.
