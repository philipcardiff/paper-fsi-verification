## 5. Interface coupling, the "M2 sensitivity" and the failed prediction (V from source)
Definition of the labelled run (`src/coupling/monolithic_fsi.py` `_m2_term`): F_s += (1 - 1/|a|) sigma_f a . phi on the interface with a = J F^-T N, so the force on the plate is sigma_f a/|a| = sigma_f n_hat(current) per REFERENCE area (the "current-area density passed per reference area" measure). It is exactly and unambiguously defined, only at L2, with M1 and M2 sharing the mesh and dt. The reported F_y is the fluid-side Nanson force and is therefore the same for both (difference 5e-5); the effect of M2 appears in the plate response (R_left_y -0.83 %, |u_y| +1.04 %). The sign of the u_y change is plausible although the total load is smaller: J_s ranges from about 0.97 at mid-span to 1.09 at the clamps (CAVITY_AUDIT section 6), so M2 removes load near the clamps and adds it at mid-span (D, mechanism not tested here).

What the tutorial applies. For `nonLinearGeometryTotalLagrangianTotalDisplacement` (the cavity `solidProperties`), `nonLinGeomTotalLagTotalDispSolid.C` builds `SfCurrent = J Finv^T & Sf`, `nCurrent`, `magSfCurrent` (lines 480-485) and `enforceTractionBoundaries` (lines 251-384) sets, for a `solidTraction` patch with `useUndeformedArea` false, `forceP = (traction - nCurrent pressure) * magSfCurrent` (line 383; the high-order branch uses `mag(cof(F) & nRef)`, lines 340-362). `useUndeformedArea` is read with default false (`solidTractionFvPatchVectorField.C:77`), and the tutorial `0/solid/D` interface patch does not set it. The fluid traction handed over is a force per unit area (`pimpleFluid::patchViscousForce` = rho nf & (-devReff), `patchPressureForce` = rho p; `fluidSolidInterface.C:1109-1125`, 2150-2210, direct 1:1 map). Hence the force on the solid is traction x CURRENT solid face area = sigma_f n da, the Nanson-consistent (M1) measure, not M2. The M1/M2 conclusion document derived its "tutorial = M2" statement from the LINEAR-geometry solid path (`linGeomTotalDispSolid.C`, undeformed normals; the blob case); the cavity tutorial does not use that path. CAVITY_AUDIT section 13 explicitly marked this as U (default of `useUndeformedArea` secondhand); it is now checked at the commit 669d0f58d and is contradicted for this tutorial. (V by source reading; no solids4foam run was performed here, so the equality of the applied and fluid-reported force in an actual s4f run is not tested; fluid and solid face areas coincide when the meshes match at the interface.)

Consequences:
- Preregistered prediction (b) ("M2 changes |u_y| by +0.3 to +1.2 % and moves it toward R") rested on the premise "tutorial = M2". The size part holds (+1.04 %); the "toward R" part fails because the premise is false: the exact M1 run already equals R to 0.05 %, and M2 moves 1 % past it. This is a mislabel of the premise, not a numerical failure and not evidence against the formulation. The results text ("Prediction (b) ... partially failed", "either the tutorial transfer is closer to M1 than assumed, or ... cancel; cannot be decided") should say that source inspection decides it: for the cavity's total-Lagrangian solid the tutorial applies current-area traction on current area, i.e. M1-equivalent, which explains the 0.05 % agreement without invoking cancellation. The preregistered band text "agrees within the expected formulation band (the tutorial uses the M2-type transfer)" is likewise void for this case.
- The statement "M2 ... Converged, same gates" (RESULTS section 3) is wrong: for M2 the relative balance residual peaks at 1.3e-2 and sits at 8.7e-3 at steady state (absolute 0.099), against the gate G4 <= 1e-8. By construction M2 is not force-conservative (the plate is loaded by sigma_f n_hat while the assembled fluid force is sigma_f a), so the gate cannot be met; the power gate G5 (|P_f - P_s|, 7e-13) still holds because the weak powers are computed with the shared form. Report M2 as "G4 not applicable by construction" rather than "same gates".
- The CAVITY_AUDIT proxy (open-loop F_y change 0.93 %) predicted the sign of the u_y effect correctly only by accident of the argument; do not cite it as validated.

## 7. Conservation, steady state, time-step independence (V, D)
- Mass: Q_in = -1 at steady state, Q_out = 0.99999975 (L1) - the 2.5e-7 balance is dV/dt of the still-moving plate; steady defect <= 4.4e-13; the transient BE defect equals its closed form -(1/dt) int J1 det(F1^-1 grad(d1-d0)) to 5e-13. - Force balance and power: assembled balance <= 3.2e-12 relative (M1), |P_f - P_s| <= 8.2e-12 absolute. Caveat (D): in a monolithic formulation with a shared test function both are algebraic identities of the residual assembly; they certify that the implementation is internally consistent, not that the constitutive law, the geometry or the coupling measure is physically right (a wrong StVK constant would pass them). The physical checks are the preflight analytic tests (T1 constant pressure, T2 Nanson length, T3 follower force), the rigid-wall limit (1.6e-7 at E x 1e5), the independent plate-only StVK value in paper-data (-0.24047 at 40 x 4 for q = 1.315, load-only, not converged in absolute terms) and, above all, the agreement with s4f.

- BE pseudo-time criterion: |change|/(|value| dt) < 1e-6 per s for u_y and weak F_y on 3 consecutive steps for t >= 60 s. It is dt-normalised (a per-step threshold of 1e-5 at dt = 10, 5e-6 at dt = 5). The residual pseudo-time error is therefore of order 1e-6 m (about 5e-6 relative), identical on all levels (the last 8 increments agree to 2 % across L2 and L4), so it cancels in the level differences used for orders and is negligible against the L3-L4 difference 1.5e-4 m. The scan L1 dt 2.5/5/10 (1.5e-5) supports this. The claim "steady state independent of dt" is therefore supported to about 2e-6 (u_y) and 6e-8 (F_y); the criterion is "steady to 5e-6 relative", not machine steady. dt = 20 diverges at the first step (kept; honest).

## 10. Honest assessment
Is it a defensible cross-code milestone for the exact model? Yes, once the edits above are made: the problem definition matches the tutorial (V), four consistent triangle meshes with exact arcs, accepted monotone sequences for u_y and F_y, dt, solver and rigid-limit checks, correct application of the declared validity rule, a reproducible pipeline, and agreement with the s4f limit to 0.015-0.06 % in u_y and 0.001-0.06 % in F_y.

What it adds beyond the paper's cavity material (four references with Richardson orders, PR #14, #18, #20, #22, #23, #27, #12, #67): (i) the first solution of the StVK plane-strain model by a different discretisation and element family (P2/P1 Taylor-Hood, monolithic, finite element on conforming triangles, curved geometry) with its own order and GCI; (ii) support that the s4f limit (-0.2407, -5.2607) is a property of the model and not a solver artefact, which is what the plate-only material-law check (PR #20) could only argue indirectly (that check, -0.24047 at 40 x 4, lies within 0.03 % of the coupled value, a consistent but not independent hint); (iii) an exactly defined M2 variant showing the size (1 %) a reference-area traction would produce, which together with the source reading (section 5) closes the earlier open question whether the tutorial applies M1 or M2; (iv) quantified error classes (time, solver, rigid limit) that the s4f data do not have; (v) a possible reading of the FEBio deficit in PR #27 (Taylor-Hood has no equal-order pressure mode) - this remains a design expectation and is not shown here. It does not add any new reference for Tukovic/ANSYS/COMSOL and does not resolve why COMSOL differs by 1.1 %.

Would a separate DRAFT supplementary PR add scientific value? Yes, modestly, if (a) it is a draft that changes no manuscript text, (b) it carries the edits in section 9, (c) it ships only the data, scripts and this audit, with the disclosure rules the paper PR #69 already uses (same lineage), and (d) it asks reviewers for a decision on whether a second implementation of the cavity belongs in the supplement. The value is a second solution that makes the cavity's reference more credible; it is not a new discovery and must not be presented as one.

Major concerns: none that invalidate the numbers. The one substantive correction is the M2 premise (section 5): the earlier conclusion that the tutorial is M2 does not carry over to this total-Lagrangian solid; this also affects the paper-level wording in `CAVITY_AUDIT.md` section 13 and in the M1/M2 conclusion for the cavity. Minor concerns: items 1-9 above; the pseudo-time stopping error; reliance on a single mesh family; s4f limits include time and stabilisation error up to about 1e-4; the u_y order 1.3 is not explained (clamp-end singularity is plausible, not tested; U).

Unsupported or too strong claims to avoid: "independent" in any sense stronger than "a second implementation by the same group and tooling lineage" (not an independent group, no external review); "second-order convergence" for u_y; "all gates passed" for M2; "tutorial = M2" for the cavity; "agreement to 0.05 %" without naming the reference variant and its spread; "Tukovic, ANSYS and COMSOL are equivalent references"; "conservation verified" as proof of physical correctness; "steady" without the 5e-6 relative statement.

Required restrictions on text: (1) independence wording as above; (2) u_y order "about 1.3 (not 2), pre-asymptotic" and F_y order "2.2 on a single accepted triple"; (3) no equivalence among Tukovic, ANSYS and COMSOL and the exact model, with COMSOL labelled StVK-in-intent but 2-D setting unconfirmed and 0.7 % higher load; (4) M2 described as a sensitivity measure that is not the tutorial's cavity transfer; (5) comparison to s4f stated with the reference variant, dx-dt coupling and criterion; (6) the milestone claims the exact model only.
