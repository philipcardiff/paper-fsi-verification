# Source-level audit of FEBio v4.13's fluid-FSI interface traction and its reconstruction from the stored CFD1 fields

Source read (FEBio tag v4.13, unmodified): `FEBioFluid/FEFluidFSITraction.cpp` (the surface load `fluid-FSI traction`), `FEBioFluid/FEFluid.cpp`, `FEElasticFluid.cpp`, `FELinearElasticFluid.cpp`, `FENewtonianFluid.cpp`, `FEBioFluid/FEFluidDomain3D.cpp`, `FEFluidFSISolver.cpp`, `FECore/FESurface.cpp`. Earlier cavity-work audits are consistent with what is stated here; every item below was re-read in the source. Nothing was modified or run.

## 1. How the interface load is assembled (CONFIRMED FROM SOURCE; line numbers of v4.13)
1. **Pressure from dilatation.** `FEFluid::Pressure(e, T)` -> `FEElasticFluid::Pressure(ef, T)` (`FEElasticFluid.cpp` l.224-233, builds a temporary material point with m_ef = e; `FEFluid.cpp` l.128) -> `FELinearElasticFluid::Pressure(mp)` = `-m_k * fp.m_ef` (`FELinearElasticFluid.cpp` l.57-63): **p = -K e_f** with the `k` of the `fluid` material (`FEFluid.cpp` l.42, 73).
2. **Nodal dilatation at the facet Gauss points** (`FEFluidFSITraction::GetFluidDilatation`, `FEFluidFSITraction.cpp` l.121-134): e_f at a facet integration point = sum over the facet nodes of H_j (alpha e_f,j + (1 - alpha) e_f,j,prev), with the facet shape functions H and the NODAL dilatation DOF (not an element or Gauss-point value of the adjacent fluid element). alpha = alphaf = 1/(1 + rhoi) (`FEFluidFSISolver.cpp` l.193): 1 for rhoi = 0, 0.5 for rhoi = 1.
3. **Viscous stress** (`GetFluidStress`, l.136-155): the first of the two elements on the interface (the fluid-FSI element) supplies the stress; `sv` = the AVERAGE over ALL Gauss points of that element of `Fluid()->GetViscous()->Stress(mp)`; one constant tensor per facet, not evaluated at the facet's integration points and not one-sided at the wall. The Newtonian law is `s = I tr(D) (kappa - 2 mu/3) + 2 mu D` (`FENewtonianFluid.cpp` l.68-80): note the -2 mu/3 tr(D) term (kappa = 0 here); the force effect of this term on the CFD1 fields is 0.010 N/m in drag.
4. **Normal and area** (`LoadVector`, l.157-197; `gt = gr ^ gs` l.177, `sv` l.181, e_f l.185, `f = gt*p - sv*gt` l.189): nodal coordinates at alphaf, `rt = alpha x_t + (1 - alpha) x_prev` (`FESurface::GetNodalCoordinates(el, alpha, rt)`, `FESurface.cpp` l.2379-2385) = the CURRENT configuration; covariant basis `gr = deriv1 * m_s`, `gs = deriv2`, `gt = gr x gs` (not normalised: |gt| is the current area element, so area changes enter the load); the sign `m_s` comes from `FESurface::FacePointing` (`FESurface.cpp` l.782-818): the sign of (surface point - element centroid) . surface normal for the attached element, so `gt` points OUT of the fluid element regardless of the facet node ordering; `m_s` is inverted if the surface has no second element (`bself`). `shell_bottom` (`m_bshellb`) only selects the shell-bottom displacement DOFs and node positions (`FESurface::GetNodalCoordinates`); it is not relevant for hex8 interfaces.
5. **Traction and nodal force:** `f = gt p - sv gt` at each Gauss point and `fa = H_a f` (times the Gauss weight inside `FESurface::LoadVector`) is added to the structure's displacement DOFs (`m_dofU`) of the facet nodes; the surface load is evaluated in the model-load loop of the residual (`FEFluidFSISolver::Residual`, `mli.LoadVector(RHS)` l.1380) and of the stiffness (`FEFluidFSISolver::StiffnessMatrix`, `ModelLoad(i)->StiffnessMatrix(LS)` l.1213). Physically: the force of the fluid on the structure, p n dA - (viscous stress) n dA with n out of the fluid.
6. **Tangent** (`FEFluidFSITraction::StiffnessMatrix`, l.199-295): derivatives with respect to u (area change through `A`, pressure p, viscous term), w (through the rate-of-deformation tangent `cv`) and e_f (`Tangent_Strain` of the elastic fluid and `svJ`), using the same element-mean `sv`, `svJ`, `cv`, `Ls`.
7. **Fluid momentum equation** (`FEFluidDomain3D::ElementInternalForce`, `FEFluidDomain3D.cpp` l.169-236): internal force `fs = sv gradN_i + gradp H_i` (l.223), i.e. the pressure enters as N_i grad(p) with the gradient of the interpolated NODAL dilatation (non-integrated-by-parts form), not as -p grad N_i. For interior nodes the two forms coincide; at wall nodes they differ by the boundary integral of p N_i n. The solver never enforces the momentum equation at no-slip wall nodes (fixed DOFs) and it does not output reactions.

## 2. Comparison with the CFD1 reconstructions
- Pressure part of the actual traction = integral over the facet of the linear NODAL interpolant of p = -K e_f times the current normal: **identical to the F1 pressure part** (exact for a straight facet; PROVEN by the formula, numerically 10.039 N/m drag, 2.809 N/m lift on C1p).
- Viscous part of the actual traction = element-mean (all Gauss points) of the full viscous stress with the -2 mu/3 tr(D) term. F1's viscous part uses D at the facet Gauss points (one-sided) and F2's the element-centre D. **Neither is exactly the actual formula**; the actual-formula reconstruction F-actual below uses the element Gauss-point mean (4 in-plane points for the z-uniform slab, equal to the 8-point mean).
- F2 uses the element-mean pressure: **not what the solver applies**.

## 3. Reconstruction from the stored CFD1 fields (`scripts/hronturek_fsi_traction_recon.py`, `results/fsi_traction_reconstruction_*.json`; C1p)
| item | drag F_x (N/m) | lift F_y (N/m) |
|---|---|---|
| pressure part (nodal facet integral) | 10.0387 | 2.8094 |
| viscous part, element Gauss-point mean, FEBio form (with the tr(D) term) | 6.4381 | 0.0649 |
| viscous part, Gauss-point mean, 2 mu D only | 6.4281 | 0.0648 |
| viscous part, element-centre D (F2 form) | 6.4279 | 0.0649 |
| **F-actual (reconstruction of the FEBio traction formula applied to the rigid-flag fields)** | **16.4768** | **2.8743** |
(F1 for comparison: 16.5413 / 2.8759.) Max |tr D| at element centres 0.76 1/s (rms 0.053).
**Can the actual coupled traction be reconstructed accurately from the stored fields?** The pressure and the element-mean viscous parts: yes, to numerical precision, for the rigid-flag fields, because (i) the nodal dilatation is stored twice and the two copies agree to 7.2e-12 (log `node_data` and xplt), (ii) the nodal velocities of the z = 0 layer are logged every step, (iii) the xplt element velocity equals the mean of the logged nodal velocities to 1.5e-08 m/s, so the slab is z-uniform and the 2-D reduction is exact for the data. What is NOT available and was not invented: the Gauss-point viscous stress and the velocity gradient are not in the xplt (only element-averaged velocity and pressure were plotted; the gradient is rebuilt from nodal velocities by the bilinear map); no FEBio output of the applied nodal force exists (reaction forces are zero in fluid-FSI and in the solid module for fixed DOFs), so the reconstruction was NOT compared with a solver-produced load (NOT TESTED); a coupled run would evaluate the formula on the moving, deflected facets with alpha-interpolated coordinates and velocities that include the mesh motion, which a rigid-flag run cannot represent; only the first-order geometry (rigid flag) and the steady state were used. Outcome: on the rigid-flag fields the applied traction would be 16.48 N/m in drag (+15.3 %) and 2.87 N/m in lift (+157 %), driven by the nodal-pressure term: the same parity mode that fails the gates in stage C.

## 4. Analytical diagnostic of the flag deflection (NOT a result)
Euler-Bernoulli plane-strain cantilever (E' = E/(1 - nu^2) = 1.667e6 Pa, I' = h^3/12, h = 0.02 m, L = 0.351 m, root clamped, linear, pressure-only transverse load of the rigid flag from C1p, applied as point loads at the facet midpoints: tip deflection = sum q_k s_k^2 (3L - s_k)/(6 E'I')). Result: nodal-pressure load 3.33 N/m net -> 11.6 mm; element-mean-pressure load 0.64 N/m -> 1.56 mm; the Featflow FSI1 value is 0.82 mm. Assumptions and limits: rigid-flag loading (the deflected flag sees a different load), pressure only (the shear on the faces is axial and ignored), linear beam theory with no shear deformation, no coupling feedback, the load distribution of the level-1 mesh. **The 11.6 mm figure is an analytical diagnostic of the size of the nodal-pressure error, not an established FEBio coupled result; no coupled simulation was run.**

## 5. Limitations
Single mesh level; the CFD1 run is steady and rigid; the F-actual viscous part needs the nodal velocities of the z = 0 layer only; pins and the K = 1e6 choice were not varied; the claim that the pressure part is the dominant error is demonstrated for the rigid-flag fields only.
